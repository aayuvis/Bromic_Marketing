Every Great Terrace Starts As A Sketch

The ones that earn all year have heat drawn in from the first line.

Outdoor seats now carry a real share of sales. Cities are keeping outdoor dining for good, travellers look for the outdoors, and new resorts are being planned around the evening terrace.

The question is no longer whether to seat guests outside, but for how many hours a year they stay comfortable – and that is where heat earns its place.

The Patio Is A Revenue Line

  • Capacity
  • Covers
  • Spend

Outdoor seating has moved from a summer bonus to a line in the plan. Most full-service operators now run one, it carries a real share of sales, and operators keep adding seats.

  • 81%+of North American full-service operators offer outdoor dining (Harris Poll for TouchBistro, a vendor survey)
  • 30%of operators expanded patio capacity in the past year; 39% plan to add private events (TouchBistro)
  • +24%Outdoor dining in Canada, March 2025–February 2026, against the year before (OpenTable)

Covered And Comfortable Wins

  • Cover
  • Warm
  • Convert

Weather is the reason guests go inside. Diners choose venues for their outdoor seats and stay longer when they are comfortable – so a roof and heat turn a weather risk into a reason to book.

  • 74%of Canadians plan to dine outdoors at least monthly in the warmer months (OpenTable / Ripple Research, n=1,502)
  • 25%of Canadians have stayed outdoors in bad weather rather than move inside
  • 127 minaverage time outdoor diners spend at the table – 20% longer than the average diner (OpenTable Canada)

Shift 03 of 10

Outdoor Dining Is Here To Stay

  • Permit
  • Winterise
  • Stay Open

Cities are keeping outdoor dining for good. New York has made roadway dining year-round, England’s pavement licences are permanent, Sydney has waived outdoor dining fees for good and Hong Kong has cut approval times from a year to about a month.

  • PermanentEngland’s pavement licences since 2024: up to two years, fee capped at £500 for a new licence
  • ~1,000outdoor dining approvals in the City of Sydney since 2020; fees now waived permanently
  • ~1 monthHong Kong outdoor seating approval for simple cases, down from a median of 12 months (April 2026)

Shift 04 of 10

Electric Becomes The Licence To Operate

  • Electrify
  • Enclose
  • Comply

Regulation is moving towards fixed, controlled, electric heat. France has banned heating open terraces, UK councils ask for infrared on pavement licences and New York’s all-electric rule for new buildings has been upheld.

  • EnclosedA heated Paris terrace must be covered, airtight and closed by rigid side walls (Ville de Paris)
  • Infraredis the condition for heaters on pavement licences in councils such as St Albans, York and Stevenage
  • July 2027New York’s all-electric rule reaches new buildings of seven storeys or more

Shift 05 of 10

Every Hour Of Heat Is Counted

  • Zone
  • Dim
  • Report

Costs are up and carbon is reported. Operators now judge heat per seat-hour – in what it costs to run and what it emits – and large hotel groups have to report it.

  • +36%Total expenses for an average US restaurant, 2019–2026 (National Restaurant Association)
  • 35%of US and Canadian operators name utilities among their steepest cost increases (SilverChef, a vendor survey)
  • FY2027Large hotel groups stay in scope of EU sustainability reporting (CSRD, after Omnibus I)

The Golden Hour Moves Earlier

  • Sunset
  • Shoulder
  • Winter

The busiest hours are moving to early evening, and the outdoor season is stretching. In Britain, 5–7pm now outsells late evening; in the Gulf, the peak season is the cool winter evening.

  • 2.04Mvisitors to Dubai in December 2025, a record month, in the cool-evening season (Dubai DET)
  • 80.7%Dubai hotel occupancy in 2025, across 154,264 rooms
  • Oct–FebRiyadh Season runs through the cool months; more than 89M visits to Saudi entertainment in 2025 (GEA)
