## 01 · The Garden Becomes A Room
**Plan · Zone · Live**
- 61% say a well-designed garden makes a house feel bigger (Argos)
- 55% have created or plan distinct zones in their outdoor space (Argos)
- 77% of British adults have a private garden, about 44 million people (HTA)

**Hero stat:** 55% are zoning their gardens like rooms (Argos, 2026)
**The Implication:** A room needs a roof, light and heat. *Architects:* draw the outdoor room into the first plan. *Builders:* run power at first fix. *Homeowners:* budget for it like a room, not furniture.
**Bromic Insight:** Heat is what turns a garden zone into a room you use in October.

## 02 · Weather Is The Barrier, Comfort Is The Answer
**Cover · Warm · Stay**
- 34% name weather as the biggest barrier (Argos); in another survey 57% say it is too cold or rainy (Furniturebox/YouGov, n=2,000)
- 65% cancel outdoor plans because of the forecast at least sometimes (Furniturebox/YouGov)
- Winter half-years are 13% wetter than 1961–1990 (Met Office State of the UK Climate 2025)

**Hero stat:** 57% – too cold or too rainy is the top reason Britons don't use outdoor space more (Furniturebox/YouGov, 2026)
**The Implication:** Lead with reliability, not summer. *Homeowners:* a covered, heated space turns "maybe" evenings into certain ones. *Hospitality:* heated, covered terraces keep covers when the forecast turns.
**Bromic Insight:** Cover keeps the rain off; radiant heat keeps people there.
*Visual: "Maybe" evening vs "Yes" evening – same garden, with and without cover and heat.*

## 03 · Hotter Summers, Longer Evenings, Wetter Shoulders
**Spring · Summer · Autumn**
- Summer 2026 hottest on record (16.5 °C mean), after 2025's record year (Met Office)
- 2016–2025 averaged 1.33 °C warmer than 1961–1990
- Autumn 2025 rainfall 120% of average (Met Office)

**Hero stat:** Two record years in a row – 2025 warmest year, 2026 hottest summer (Met Office)
**The Implication:** Gardens get more use, and the season stretches either side. *Designers:* plan shade for summer and heat and rain cover from October to April. *Hospitality:* trade the terrace earlier in spring and later in autumn.
**Bromic Insight:** Warmer years bring more evenings outside; heat makes the cooler ones count.

## 04 · The Zoned Garden And The Outdoor Kitchen
**Cook · Dine · Gather**
- Dining zones are the most popular garden zone (Argos)
- John Lewis outdoor cooking sales +42% (Oct 2025 YoY)
- Garden centre BBQs and heating +25% (July 2026, HTA)

**Hero stat:** +25% – BBQs and heating, the fastest-moving garden centre category (HTA, July 2026)
**The Implication:** Every zone needs its own heat and its own switch. *Designers:* specify heat per zone. *Homeowners:* heat the dining table, not the lawn.
**Bromic Insight:** Zone heat where people sit, controlled zone by zone.

## 05 · Electric-First Outdoor Heat
**Electrify · License · Comply**
- The Future Homes Standard was laid in March 2026: clean heating as standard in new homes, with gas boilers effectively phased out (NHBC)
- Councils including St Albans, York, Stevenage and North Somerset allow only electric or infrared heaters on pavement licences; gas is not permitted
- UK grid carbon intensity 126 g/kWh in 2025; renewables 47% of supply (Carbon Brief)

**Hero stat:** 47% – renewables' share of UK electricity in 2025 (Carbon Brief)
**The Implication:** *Builders and architects:* new homes increasingly have no gas supply, so outdoor heat is electric. *Hospitality:* pavement licences increasingly require infrared. *Homeowners:* switch-on heat, no cylinders.
**Bromic Insight:** Electric infrared fits the licence, the new home and a cleaner grid.
*Visual: map of councils requiring infrared on pavement licences.*

## 06 · Heat People, Not Air
**Target · Dim · Time**
- Electricity cap 26.32p/kWh (Oct–Dec 2026, Ofgem)
- Energy is a top worry for 80% of hospitality members; 74% saw higher electricity bills (UKHospitality, BBPA, BII survey, Oct 2026)
- Government-commissioned research: infrared comfort depends on line of sight – it heats people and surfaces, not air (DESNZ / Leeds Sustainability Institute, 2025)

**Hero stat:** 80% of hospitality businesses name energy costs as a top worry (Oct 2026)
**The Implication:** Running cost is the question; placement and control are the answer. *Designers:* place heaters with clear line of sight to seats. *Operators:* zone, dim and time heat to covers.
**Bromic Insight:** Infrared warms the people it can see; good placement and Smart-Heat controls do the rest.

## 07 · Smaller, Paved, Higher
**Compact · Terrace · Balcony**
- 42% of domestic garden area in Great Britain is paved; 36% of back gardens (RHS State of Gardening 2025)
- Only 61–63% of renters have a private plot, vs 92% of owners (English Housing Survey 2024–25)
- Flats are 27% of new completions in Wales (2025-26)

**Hero stat:** 42% of garden area is paved – the patio is the British outdoor room (RHS, 2025)
**The Implication:** *Designers:* wall, ceiling and recessed heat keeps small terraces free. *Developers:* heated terraces and balconies sell flats. *Homeowners:* one heater turns a paved patio into an evening room.
**Bromic Insight:** Wall and ceiling heat warms small spaces without taking floor space.

## 08 · The Pub Garden Protects The Margin
**Covers · Terrace · Season**
- Managed pubs +9.1% like-for-like in April 2025 as warm weather drew people into gardens; pubs +4.2% in July 2026 (NIQ/RSM)
- About 360 pubs forecast to close in 2026 (BBPA)
- Pavement licences permanent in England since March 2024; heaters named as permitted furniture

**Hero stat:** +9.1% – pub sales when the garden fills (NIQ/RSM, April 2025)
**The Implication:** Outdoor space wins when it is usable. *Operators:* heated, covered gardens win covers in spring and autumn, not just heatwaves. *Designers:* fixed heat with metering and controls.
**Bromic Insight:** A heated pub garden earns in March and October, not only in July.

## 09 · Considered, Better Projects
**Considered · Designed · Lasting**
- Garden centre sales +9% in 2025 (HTA); garden centre card spend +4.0% (Barclays, July 2026)
- 50% of renovating homeowners worked on outdoor space; median renovation spend £21,440 (Houzz UK 2025)
- 23% of renovators added outdoor lighting, 11% gazebos or pergolas (Houzz UK)

**Hero stat:** 50% of UK renovators work on outdoor space (Houzz, 2025)
**The Implication:** Expect better-specified projects led by designers and architects. Lead with quality, design and controls.
**Bromic Insight:** A considered garden deserves heat designed into it.
