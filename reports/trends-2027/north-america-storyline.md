## 01 · The Outdoor Room Enters The Floor Plan
**Plan · Build · Live**

The outdoor room is now drawn in at the start. Architects rank outdoor living first among special-function rooms, blended indoor-outdoor space keeps climbing, and more than half of renovators are already working outside.

- Outdoor living is the most-requested special-function room (20% of architects name it first)
- Blended indoor-outdoor space: net 43%, up from 40%
- 71% of outdoor renovators now hire a professional (up from 65% in 2024)

**Hero stat:** Net 51% of architects report rising demand for outdoor rooms (AIA, Q2 2026 data)
**The Implication:** A room on the plan needs the services of a room: power, light and heat, specified with the structure. *Architects:* draw heat on the reflected ceiling plan. *Builders:* run the circuit at frame stage. *Homeowners:* ask for heat in the first quote, alongside lighting.
**Bromic Insight:** Heat designed in from day one disappears into the architecture and turns an outdoor room into a room you use every evening.
*Visual: plan drawing morphing into a finished heated terrace.*

## 02 · Comfort Is The New Amenity
**Gather · Linger · Host**

The lounge has become the heart of the outdoor room, and warmth is what keeps people in it. Fire and heat already sit in almost half of new lounges and dining areas, and Americans plan to host more at home.

- 83% of redesigned outdoor spaces include a lounge
- 48% of those lounges, and 49% of dining areas, include a fire feature
- 28% of Americans expect to host more at home in 2026 – 47% of Gen Z

**Hero stat:** 38% of homeowners plan to add fire pits or outdoor heating this year (Talker Research for Trex, 2025, n=2,000)
**The Implication:** Comfort is now part of the brief, measured in how long guests stay. *Homeowners:* plan heat for where people sit, not where the fire pit looks best. *Designers:* zone warmth over lounge and dining. *Builders:* offer a heated lounge as a named upgrade.
**Bromic Insight:** Radiant heat warms people directly, so the conversation keeps going after the sun has gone.
*Visual: thermal-style overlay showing warm seating zones on a lounge photo.*

## 03 · Heated Space Returns To The Architect's Brief
**Specify · Enclose · Extend**

Architects are asking for spaces that work beyond summer. Three-season porches recorded the biggest jump of any room, and heated exterior space is rising again.

- Sunrooms and three-season porches: net 25%, up from net 6% – the largest gain of any room
- Heated exterior space: net 16%, up from 12%
- Infrared patio heaters hold steady among architects (net 16%)

**Hero stat:** Net 25% – three-season rooms are the fastest-rising room type (AIA, Q2 2026 data)
**The Implication:** "Three-season" becomes "four-season" with heat. *Architects:* specify heating as part of the porch package. *Builders:* price the heated porch as one line item. *Homeowners:* a heated covered porch adds months of use for a fraction of an addition's cost.
**Bromic Insight:** Infrared heat is the simplest way to turn a three-season room into a four-season one.
*Visual: bar chart of the AIA jumps, with the three-season porch highlighted in flame.*

## 04 · Cover First, Then Comfort
**Shelter · Mount · Integrate**

Shade structures are the fastest-growing outdoor project, and every new roof is a mounting surface. Outdoor kitchens are going under cover and getting real infrastructure.

- Shade structures rose 15 points to 35% of structure upgrades
- 74% of outdoor kitchens are fully or partly covered
- 68% of outdoor kitchen projects add plumbing, electrical or heating infrastructure (up 10 points)

**Hero stat:** +15 points – shade structures are the breakout project of 2026 (Houzz Outdoor Trends Study, n=1,191)
**The Implication:** The roof, the power and the heat are one decision. *Pergola and patio-cover makers:* design in mounting points and power runs. *Architects:* recess or ceiling-mount heat to keep the roofline clean. *Builders:* rough-in heater circuits with lighting circuits.
**Bromic Insight:** Every pergola, louvre roof and covered patio is a mounting surface waiting for heat.
*Visual: exploded pergola diagram – roof, light, heat, controls.*

## 05 · The Longer Shoulder Season
**Spring · Fall · Evening**

The outdoor season is getting longer. Falls are warmer across almost every US city, and Canada is warming at nearly twice the global rate. More evenings are now mild enough to be outdoors, as long as the chill that comes after sunset is handled.

- Falls warmer in 241 of 245 US cities since 1970 (+2.9°F on average)
- 88% of cities gained at least a week of above-normal fall days
- Americans spent 11.7 hours a week outdoors at home in 2025, up from 9.6

**Hero stat:** 98% of US cities have warmer falls than in 1970 (Climate Central, 2026)
**The Implication:** The opportunity sits in the evenings either side of summer. *Homeowners:* heat turns September–November evenings into usable ones. *Hospitality:* open the patio earlier in spring and keep it open later in fall. *Builders:* market outdoor rooms as all-season.
**Bromic Insight:** Warmer seasons bring more evenings outside; heat makes the hours after sunset count.
*Visual: calendar ring showing summer widening into spring and fall, with evening hours lit.*

## 06 · Year-Round Patios Become Policy
**Permit · Winterize · Stay Open**

Cities are making outdoor dining permanent. New York now allows year-round roadway cafés and winter coverings, Toronto runs about 1,500 outdoor dining spaces, and Canadians are booking outdoors more than ever.

- New York signed year-round outdoor dining into law (Int. 655, August 2026)
- New York permit filings peaked at 475 a month in July 2026; about 1,800 setups eligible
- Canadian outdoor dining reservations up 24%; outdoor diners stay 20% longer (127 min)

**Hero stat:** 12 months – New York's outdoor dining season, by law, from 2026
**The Implication:** A year-round permit needs year-round comfort. *Operators:* plan heat into the winterized structure from day one. *Designers:* work within fire-code rules for enclosures and heaters. *Cities:* expect heated, covered patios to become the norm.
**Bromic Insight:** Fixed, code-compliant electric heat makes a year-round patio possible without tanks to refill.
*Visual: map of New York and Toronto with outdoor-dining pins glowing through winter.*

## 07 · The Patio Protects The Margin
**Seats · Stay · Spend**

With costs high and traffic soft, operators are counting on the patio. Outdoor seats extend capacity, guests linger longer, and diners actively choose restaurants with outdoor seating.

- 42% of US operators were not profitable in 2025 (National Restaurant Association)
- 62% of Canadians choose a restaurant for its outdoor seating (OpenTable, n=1,502)
- Catering and hospitality account for 58% of the outdoor heating market (Fortune Business Insights, 2026)

**Hero stat:** 20% longer – how much longer outdoor diners stay at the table (OpenTable Canada, 2026)
**The Implication:** Every heated outdoor seat adds capacity without adding floor space. *Operators:* treat heating as revenue infrastructure with a measurable payback. *Hospitality designers:* zone heat by table and bar. *Hotels:* rooftops and terraces are the beverage trade-up spaces (NIQ, 2026).
**Bromic Insight:** A heated seat earns more nights each year than an unheated one.
*Visual: covers-by-month chart – unheated patio vs heated patio.*

## 08 · Electric Comes Of Age
**Electrify · Pre-Wire · Comply**

Homes and cities are moving to electric. Heat pumps out-shipped gas furnaces again in 2025, New York's all-electric buildings law was upheld, and electric is the fastest-growing type of outdoor heater.

- 3.64M heat pumps vs 3.25M gas furnaces shipped in the US in 2025 (AHRI)
- New York's All-Electric Buildings Act upheld by the 2nd Circuit (June 2026); Montreal bars networked gas appliances in new buildings
- Electric is the fastest-growing outdoor-heating fuel type; gas and propane still hold 54% (Fortune, 2026)

**Hero stat:** Fastest-growing – electric outdoor heating (Fortune Business Insights, 2026)
**The Implication:** Plan capacity now and the choice of fuel stays open. *Builders and electricians:* size panels and run outdoor circuits at rough-in, to NEC 2026. *Architects:* specify electric where gas is restricted. *Homeowners:* no tanks, no flame, instant heat at the switch.
**Bromic Insight:** Electric infrared fits the all-electric home, and we offer gas where it still suits.
*Visual: split image – propane tank fading out, wall switch lighting a heater.*

## 09 · Small Lots, Big Living
**Compact · Zone · Elevate**

Lots are small and patios are getting tighter, but buyers want outdoor living anyway – designed with purpose. Multigenerational living adds more people to the same outdoor space.

- Median new-home lot: 8,543 sq ft; 64% of lots under 9,000 sq ft (NAHB, 2025)
- Patio share of new homes at a six-year low: 60.5%
- Accommodations for multiple generations: net 53% among architects

**Hero stat:** 60.5% – new homes with a patio, the lowest in six years, so every square foot must work harder (NAHB/Census, 2026)
**The Implication:** Compact spaces need heat that takes no floor space. *Builders:* make the small patio the selling feature – covered, lit, heated. *Designers:* use wall, ceiling and recessed heat to keep the floor free. *Homeowners:* one well-placed heater can make a small terrace a nightly room.
**Bromic Insight:** Wall, ceiling and recessed heat warm the people, not the footprint.
*Visual: small rooftop or balcony at night with a slim wall-mounted heater.*

## 10 · Smart, Quiet, Integrated
**Control · Scene · Simplify**

Half of US internet households now own a smart-home device, and architects keep asking for technology-friendly homes. Outdoors, smart wins where it gives real control.

- 49% of US internet households own at least one smart-home device (Parks Associates, 2026)
- Technology-friendly systems: net 51% among architects; energy-management systems net 34%
- Smart security cameras outsell standard 25% to 3% among renovators – outdoors, people pay for control

**Hero stat:** 49% – smart homes are already the norm (Parks Associates, 2026)
**The Implication:** Heat joins lighting and audio in one scene. *Integrators:* add heating zones to the outdoor scene. *Homeowners:* dimmable, zoned heat on a schedule or an app. *Hospitality:* set zones by table and time.
**Bromic Insight:** Affinity Smart-Heat controls dim, zone and schedule heat with the rest of the home. App and Wi-Fi features need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately.

## Closing · From Optional To Essential: Plan Heat From The Start
Outdoor heating is following the path of the built-in dishwasher, the built-in kitchen and the built-in grill. The outdoor room is on the plan, the season is longer, cities are keeping patios open, and homes are going electric. Every one of these shifts leads to the same conclusion: heat belongs in the first drawing.

**The market is growing:** the global outdoor heating market is estimated at US$1.48B in 2026, rising to US$2.47B by 2034 (6.6% a year); North America is the largest region at 45.7% (Fortune Business Insights, 2026).
**The gap is the opportunity:** in 2026, fewer than 1 in 4 homeowners prioritised heating at planning (Bromic Outdoor Living Trends 2026, Atomik Research).

**What early planning enables**
- Circuits and capacity sized at rough-in (NEC 2026)
- Heat recessed or mounted cleanly into roofs, pergolas and walls
- One control scene for light, sound and heat
- More months, more evenings, more covers
