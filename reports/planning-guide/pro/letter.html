Question 01 · Map The Heat

Map The Heat Before The Ceiling Fills Up

Mark where people sit, dine, stand and pass, then lay the heat over those zones before choosing a finish or a mount. Heaters compete for the soffit with downlights, fans, speakers and ducts – coordinate them in design development.

  • About 20 ft² A SeatMeasure the seating and bar rails, not the circulation.
  • Fewer, Higher-OutputFewer heaters placed where they work read as a calmer ceiling.
  • Match The FinishBlack, white, stainless – and graphite in the coming Select collection.

Question 02

Confirm The Space Is Outdoors

For heating design a space is outdoor when at least 30% of its horizontal perimeter is permanently open. A cover holds a gentle heat cloud; sizing always rests on radiant coverage.

Mount By Surface

Question 03 · The Recess

Detail The Recess As Its Own Drawing

A recessed heater gives warmth felt without being seen and keeps the soffit line clean. Recessing is an electric detail; built-in gas mounts to the surface.

Series II low-clearance kit (Select 304, Coastal 316 marine stainless): figures from its installation instructions. Draw each recess with the kit and cut-out, cavity above the finished ceiling, blocking, junction-box position and access, and the clearances the manual sets.

Question 04

State The Cover And The Climate

The same heater covers less in colder air, and wind draws heat out of the zone. The sizing model states its factors so the count is traceable.

  • CoverOpen 0.85 · partly covered 1.0 · covered 1.1
  • ClimateMild 1.0 · cool 0.85 · cold 0.7
  • FreestandingNever more than four seats each

Exposed site? Add glass screens or windbreaks on the windward side as part of the heating design.

Question 04 · Height And Placement

Set Heights By How The Zone Is Used

Radiant intensity falls with the square of distance. Too high and the output is lost before it arrives; too low and the seat beneath runs hot.

A wall heater projects its warmth outward: a table pushed against the wall beneath it sits in a dead zone. Set the table forward, and warm wide tables from both sides.

Heights by use: Bromic guidance (AIA/CES course), never below each model’s minimum. Confirm against each installation manual.

How Heat Moves Outdoors

Every Outdoor Scheme Rests On Radiant Warmth

Convected heat rises and disperses, and forced air cannot hold an open space at temperature. Directional infrared aimed at people is what works.

Short Wave

Hottest elements; instant, intense, bright glow and glare.

Medium Wave

Soft glow, little glare, warm in about 30 seconds. Bromic Platinum, Eclipse and Tungsten Electric.

Long Wave

No visible glow, about 5 minutes to warm, more easily dispersed by moving air.

All infrared travels in straight lines at the speed of light; reach depends on output, reflector and mounting height, not on wavelength. Wien’s law, λ ≈ 2,898 µm·K ÷ T: about 2,400 K → 1.2 µm; 1,200 K → 2.4 µm; 700 K → 4.1 µm (typical element temperatures, for illustration). Warm-up times: Bromic Planning Guide v1.2.

06
Question 06 · Power

Size Every Branch Circuit

Current is watts ÷ volts. Fixed electric space-heating loads are continuous, so conductors and overcurrent devices are sized at no less than 125% of the load (NEC 424, 210.19(A)(1), 210.20(A)).

Gas Supply

Size piping for the total connected Btu/h, length and pressure to NFPA 54. Built-in gas also needs 110 V, 2 A at each heater; the freestanding gas heater runs on a 20 lb LPG cylinder or a 10 ft natural-gas quick-connect.

Published amps from the product pages; Tungsten rows are W ÷ V and marked. Breaker sizes, Article 424 vs 422 and Tungsten amps await confirmation against the installation manuals. Feeders and services follow Article 220.

Question 05 · Controls

Specify The Controls With Every Heater

Zoning is the efficiency strategy: one channel per zone that opens on its own, each at its own level. Every heater schedule carries a controls line.

Controllers IP54, −4 to 86 °F; 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, Bluetooth about 30 ft, optional Ethernet; UL 60730-1. Wi-Fi and app features need compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately. Control4, Crestron and Lutron compatibility varies and may need extra hardware.

Question 06 · Rough-In

Power In Before The Ceiling Closes

Plan the rough-in while the ceiling is open and the wiring goes in once – hidden, and exactly where each heater hangs.

Rough-In Checklist
  • Heater tags on the RCP, with zone and channel
  • Blocking and recess framing, cavity depth checked
  • Circuit per heater or group at 125%; panel capacity
  • Junction boxes dimensioned, with access
  • Controller location, power and data runs; wall-switch boxes at the thresholds
  • Gas line, shut-offs and 110 V at each built-in gas heater

Clearances And Code

Confirm Clearances And Code Early

Each model is read on its own manual and listing, never interpolated from another. A heater outside its clearances can underheat the zone as well as breach the listing.

  • ElectricANSI/NFPA 70: circuits, overcurrent protection, disconnects, wiring methods.
  • GasANSI Z223.1/NFPA 54: location, supply, ventilation, clearance to combustibles.
  • CertificationAn Intertek, UL or CSA mark on every gas and electrical appliance.

Indicative only. The adopted code edition and local amendments govern. Confirm with the local building authority, and have heaters installed by a licensed electrician or gas fitter to the manufacturer’s instructions.
