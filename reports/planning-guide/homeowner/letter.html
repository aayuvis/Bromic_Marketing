Question 01

Where Do People Sit?

Warmth is for people, not floors. Measure where everyone actually gathers – around the table, the sofa or the fire – not the whole deck.

Rule of thumb: allow about 20 ft² a seat, chairs and breathing room included.

Question 02

How Open Is It?

Radiant heat is made for the outdoors. A space counts as outdoor when at least 30% of its perimeter is permanently open – and a roof works in your favor.

Question 02 · Cover

A Roof Keeps Warmth In

A Roof Keeps Warmth In

Under a cover, warm air gathers into a gentle “heat cloud” that adds to the radiant warmth, so a covered space needs fewer heaters than an open one.

Block The Wind, Keep It Open

A screen, wall or planting on the windward side helps your heaters work harder. Thinking of glass or screens all round? Talk to a designer first – it changes the design.

Question 03

What Can The Heat Hang From?

Look up and behind the seats. What is there decides the mount – and the mount decides the heater.

  • Aim At Shoulders And LapsEvery Bromic heater is directional: face it at the seats, not the table top or the floor.
  • Higher Spreads, But GentlerMount higher and the warmth spreads wider but feels softer; under a tall ceiling, bring heaters down on poles.
  • Keep The Line Of Sight ClearInfrared travels in straight lines, so an umbrella, beam or planter casts a cold shadow.

Question 04

How Cold Are Your Evenings?

Cover and cold are the two big levers. The same 12-seat dining area needs fewer heaters under a roof, and a few more on cold nights.

  • MildEvenings usually above about 50°F
  • CoolAbout 40–50°F
  • ColdOften below about 40°F

Not sure? Plan for partly covered and cool – it is the safe middle.

How Radiant Heat Works

Warmth Like Sunshine

Step into winter sun and you feel warm at once, though the air is cool. Infrared heat works the same way: it warms you, the furniture and the floor – not the air that drifts away.

Short Wave

The hottest elements. Instant, intense, with a bright glow and glare.

Medium Wave

A soft glow with little glare, warm in about 30 seconds. Bromic Platinum, Eclipse and Tungsten Electric are medium wave.

Long Wave

No visible glow, about 5 minutes to warm up, gentle – and more easily dispersed by moving air.

All infrared travels in straight lines at the speed of light. How far warmth reaches depends on the heater’s output, reflector and mounting height, not on its wavelength. Peak wavelength by Wien’s law, λ ≈ 2,898 µm·K ÷ T: about 2,400 K → 1.2 µm; 1,200 K → 2.4 µm; 700 K → 4.1 µm; the sun, about 5,800 K → 0.5 µm (typical element temperatures, for illustration). 05
Question 05 · The Fuel

Electric Or Gas

Electric

  • Covered patios, pergolas, loggias
  • Ceilings, walls, poles
  • On/off or dimming, zones, schedules, app
  • Hard-wired by a licensed electrician
  • 65–160 ft² a heater

Gas

  • Larger, more open areas
  • Built-in: wall or ceiling. Or freestanding
  • On/off, wall switch, schedules
  • A licensed gas fitter, plus a 110 V supply for built-in
  • 160–215 ft² a heater

Question 06

Power In Before The Ceiling Closes

Plan the power while the ceiling is open and the wiring goes in once – hidden, and exactly where the heaters hang.

  • ElectricHard-wired by a licensed electrician, who sizes each circuit from the heater’s current draw (in the Pro edition and the cut sheets).
  • GasBuilt-in gas needs a gas line from a licensed gas fitter and a 110 V supply. The freestanding gas heater runs on a 20 lb propane cylinder or a natural-gas quick-connect.
  • Built To CodeElectric to NFPA 70, gas to NFPA 54. Confirm with your local building authority; each manual sets its clearances.
