01 · HOW TO USE THIS KIT

The Right Home In MasterFormat

Written in the right section, the heater you chose is the heater that is built, through value engineering and bid. We recommend 23 83 33 Electric Radiant Heaters, and the reasoning below supports that choice in a spec review.

HOW TO USE THIS KIT

Inside is a CSI three-part guide specification for Bromic Smart-Heat electric radiant heaters under MasterFormat 23 83 33, Electric Radiant Heaters, a coverage and selection table, the details to draw and the CAD/BIM files to request. Edit the bracketed text, remove what does not apply, and send the coverage plan to the Bromic design team before you issue for bid.

WHY 23 83 33

  1. Division 23 (HVAC), 23 83 00 Radiant Heating Units is MasterFormat's home for equipment that heats by radiation.3 Its level-3 children are 23 83 13 (radiant heating electric cables and mats), 23 83 23 (radiant heating electric panels) and 23 83 33 (electric radiant heaters).4 A Bromic heater is a self-contained electric infrared emitter, which places it most naturally in 23 83 33.
  2. 23 82 39 Unit Heaters covers convection units (cabinet, propeller, wall and ceiling unit heaters) that move warm air.7 Outdoors, warm air rises and drifts away on the breeze, and radiant heat warms people and surfaces directly,2 so this section is a poor match.
  3. 23 55 23 Gas-Fired Radiant Heaters is the home for gas radiant equipment.6 If a project also uses Bromic gas heaters, write them there as a separate section.
  4. Industry precedent. Other manufacturers' guide specifications for outdoor electric radiant heaters use 23 83 33.5 Some product directories also tag infrared heaters under 23 82 39, a directory convention that need not shape your specification.

PART 1 – GENERAL

1.1 SUMMARY

A.Section includes:

1.Electric infrared radiant heaters for outdoor and semi-outdoor spaces: recessed, ceiling-mounted, wall-mounted and pole-suspended.

2.Heater controls: controllers, wall switches and app-based scheduling. Controls are part of the heating system and are required.

3.Mounting accessories: recess kits and suspension poles.

B.Related requirements:

1.Division 06 / 09: soffit framing, blocking and finishes at recessed units.

2.Division 26: branch circuits, raceways, disconnects, wiring devices.

3.Section 23 09 00: building automation interface, where used.

4.Section 23 55 23: gas-fired radiant heaters, where used.

1.2 REFERENCES

A.NFPA 70, National Electrical Code (NEC), edition adopted by the authority having jurisdiction (AHJ), including Article 424, Fixed Electric Space-Heating Equipment.8

B.Product safety listing by a Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory (NRTL): UL, CSA or Intertek (ETL).2 Listing standard and file numbers per heater: To confirm

C.Ingress protection rating per IEC 60529. IP rating per series: To confirm

D.Local building, energy and fire codes as adopted by the AHJ. Commercial energy codes may require outdoor heating to be radiant and controlled by an occupancy sensor or timer; see the Bromic electrification guide (A12).

Controls, Accessories, Execution

2.3 CONTROLS (REQUIRED)

A.Every heater shall be controlled by Affinity Smart-Heat Controls by Bromic Heating. A heater without its controller is not a complete system and shall not be accepted.1

B.Controllers, as scheduled on the drawings:

1.[Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off controller] – on/off control of one or more heaters per zone.

2.[Affinity Smart-Heat Dimmer controller] – variable output for Platinum and Tungsten heaters. Compatibility per series and wattage: To confirm

3.[Affinity Smart-Heat Eclipse controller] – heat steps and rim lighting for Eclipse heaters.

4.Manual wall switch at each zone, mounted at [__] in AFF, in a location visible from the heated area.

C.App: Bromic Smart-Heat app for zones, schedules and timers. Wi-Fi connectivity and app features require compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately.1 Provide Wi-Fi coverage at controller locations. Network requirements: To confirm

D.Integrations: where scheduled, interface with [Amazon Alexa] [Google Assistant] [Control4] [Crestron] [Lutron]. Compatibility varies by system; integration requires compatible Affinity controls and may require additional integration hardware or configuration.1 Coordinate with the integrator before rough-in.

E.Energy code compliance: where the AHJ requires outdoor heating to switch off automatically when unoccupied, provide [occupancy sensor] [time switch] control. Whether Affinity schedules and timers meet a timer-switch requirement, and the occupancy-sensor interface: To confirm

F.Zoning: separate zones for areas used at different times (for example dining, bar, lounge).

2.4 ACCESSORIES

A.Recess kits: manufacturer's recess kit for flush mounting of Platinum and Tungsten electric heaters in ceiling and soffit cavities.2 Part numbers, cut-out sizes and cavity depth per model: To confirm

B.Suspension poles: manufacturer's suspension poles to bring heaters to effective height under high ceilings.2 Available lengths: To confirm

C.Wall and ceiling brackets: as supplied with each heater.

PART 3 – EXECUTION

3.1 EXAMINATION

A.Verify that blocking, recess cavities and structure are in place and sized to the manufacturer's cut-out and cavity dimensions.

B.Verify that branch circuits, disconnects and control wiring are in place, labelled and match the approved shop drawings.

C.Verify clearances to combustible materials, sprinkler heads, lighting, fans, drapes and awnings before mounting. Do not proceed until conditions are corrected.

3.2 INSTALLATION

A.Install heaters, controls and accessories per the manufacturer's current installation instructions and the approved shop drawings.

B.Mounting heights: not less than the manufacturer's minimums for the model and wattage. [Bromic's planning guidance has been a minimum of 72 in above floor for wall mounting and 96 in for ceiling mounting, varying with output.2 Confirm against the current installation manual for each model.] Current minimums per model and wattage: To confirm

C.Aim wall-mounted heaters at the occupied zone, not at walkways or glazing.

D.Recessed units: install recess kit before ceiling finish; seal the ceiling penetration per the manufacturer's instructions.

3.3 CLEARANCES

A.Maintain the manufacturer's minimum clearances to ceilings, walls, combustible materials and other fixtures, and NEC 424.13.8

B.Show clearances on the shop drawings and confirm on site before final fixing.

3.4 ELECTRICAL

A.Electrical work by an electrician licensed in the project's jurisdiction, per NFPA 70 as adopted by the AHJ.

B.Provide dedicated branch circuits sized per NEC Article 424 (branch-circuit conductors not less than 125% of the heating load) and the manufacturer's instructions.8

C.Use wiring methods, boxes and devices suitable for damp or wet locations as the location requires.

D.Provide a disconnecting means as required by NEC Article 424.

E.Run control wiring and network per the controller manufacturer's requirements.

3.5 COMMISSIONING

A.Energise each zone; confirm every heater reaches operating output and every controller step (on/off, dimmer levels, Eclipse heat steps and rim lighting) responds.

B.Confirm the wall switch overrides as intended.

C.Set up the Smart-Heat app: zones named to match the drawings, schedules and timers per the Owner's operating hours.

D.Test any third-party integration end to end.

E.Record measured voltage and current per circuit in the commissioning report.

3.6 DEMONSTRATION

A.Train the Owner's staff on site [one hour] [two hours]: operation, zones, schedules, wall switch, app, cleaning and safe distances.

B.Hand over O&M manuals, warranty documents and the commissioning report.

Protecting Your Design Intent Through Bid

Whether the specification is proprietary or "or approved equal", the aim is the same: the outdoor room your client approved is the one that opens.

OPTION A · PROPRIETARY

No Substitutions

Protects the design intent through bid by limiting substitution. Common on private residential and hospitality work.

OPTION B · BASE BID WITH SUBSTITUTION

Equal, At A Higher Standard

Many public owners, and some private ones, require competition, so an architect may need to allow substitutions. Option B keeps Bromic as basis of design, sets performance criteria and asks for a coverage drawing for any substitute, so alternatives are allowed at a higher standard.

06 · CHECKLIST AND NEXT STEP

Your Planning Checklist

  1. 01Space confirmed as outdoor (at least 30% of the perimeter permanently open).2
  2. 02Heater series chosen by use: seated, standing, lounge; coastal exposure noted.
  3. 03Coverage plan (D4) drawn over the furniture layout and checked by Bromic's design team.
  4. 04Controls scheduled for every zone: controller type, wall switch, app, any integration.
  5. 05Mounting heights and clearances taken from the current installation manual for each model.
  6. 06Recess cavities and blocking shown on the architectural sections (D1).
  7. 07Circuits, voltage and disconnects coordinated with the electrical engineer.
  8. 08Energy code check: does the AHJ require occupancy or timer control for outdoor heat?
