01 · WITH YOUR CLIENTS

Positioning Outdoor Heat With Your Clients

Only 23% of homeowners prioritize outdoor heating at the planning stage.11 Raise it in the first conversation, and heat goes into the drawing, the pre-wire and the first budget.

THE EXPERIENCE

94% of homeowners say their outdoor space could be used more, and 67% say heating extends outdoor use.11

Designed in, the heater sits flush in the soffit on its own circuit, controlled alongside the lights. Added later, it is bolted to the soffit and fed by surface conduit.

ASK YOUR CLIENT

  • How and when will you use the space?
  • How many seats, seated or standing?
  • Roof, pergola or open sky?
  • Power or gas on site?
  • Should the heater be seen, or disappear?

08 · GUIDE SPECIFICATION, CONDENSED

Section 23 83 33 Electric Radiant Heaters

PART 1 · GENERAL

1.1Electric infrared radiant heaters for outdoor and semi-outdoor spaces, with their controls and mounting accessories. Controls are required.

1.2NFPA 70 (NEC) Article 424 as adopted by the AHJ.8 Listing by an NRTL (UL, CSA or Intertek) for the location.2

1.3Submit product data, shop drawings with coverage zones, heights, clearances and recess details, finish samples, listings and O&M data.

PART 2 · PRODUCTS

2.1Basis of design: Bromic Heating. Substitutions: [none] [only with equal performance and a coverage drawing for the same layout].

2.2Heaters, outputs, finishes and mounting as the equipment schedule.

2.3Controls: an Affinity Smart-Heat controller for each zone, and a manual wall switch.

2.4Recess kits, suspension poles and brackets by the manufacturer.

PART 3 · EXECUTION

3.1Install to the current manual and the shop drawings; heights and clearances not less than its minimums.

3.2Electrical work by a licensed electrician, on dedicated branch circuits per NEC Article 424.

3.3Commission each zone, set the app schedules and train the Owner’s staff.
