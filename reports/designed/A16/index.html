Before You Begin

That Evening Starts At Rough‑In

Picture your buyers on their first evening at home, stepping outside after dinner to a patio that is already warm. That evening starts at rough-in, with a few circuits, boxes and blocking that cost little while the ceiling is open and save a callback later.

At A Glance

Run a dedicated branch circuit for each electric heater (or heater group, per the controller), sized from I = W ÷ V with conductors at 125% of the load.5 Add a junction box and solid blocking at every heater, pole or recess location, a home run from each heater to the controller location, a box at the wall switch and, for built-in gas, a stubbed and capped line with an accessible shut-off.

Settle all of it before framing.

Plan It While The Ceiling Is Open

Designed To Fit. Built To Perform.

Fewer than one in four homeowners prioritize heating at planning.1 Many of the rest ask for it once the drywall, soffits and stone are finished, and by then it means opening a finished ceiling, running surface conduit, placing the heater wherever the wire reaches instead of over the table, and a callback.

Flush in the ceiling – heaters recessed into a porch ceiling, the detail buyers notice in the model home.

When It Is Decided

The later heat is planned, the more it costs to put in.

IAt design or pre-framing

A line on the electrical plan, with blocking and boxes at rough-in.

IIAfter framing, before close-in

A change order and an extra trip for the electrician.

IIIAfter finish

Opened ceilings or walls, patching, repainting, surface conduit, and often no recess option.

A heater planned at the drawing stage sits exactly over the table, flush in a clean ceiling, with nothing to patch.
Offer It At The Options Stage

A pre-wire package of circuits, boxes, blocking and a switch box can be sold even when the heaters are bought later, so your buyers move into an outdoor space that is ready for warmth whenever they choose. The builder program and its Good–Better–Best guide show how (A15).

Circuits Sized Right The First Time

One That Never Trips On The First Cold Night

A circuit sized correctly is one that never trips on the first cold night, and it takes three steps. Fixed electric space-heating equipment is treated as a continuous load.

125%

NEC Article 424 requires its branch-circuit conductors to be sized at not less than 125% of the load, consistent with the continuous-load rules in 210.19(A)(1) and 210.20(A).5,4 Feeder and service calculations follow Article 220.

1Find the current per heater

I = W ÷ V

2Allow for continuous load

Branch-circuit conductors at not less than 125% of the load.

3Choose the circuit and breaker

The electrician selects conductor and overcurrent device from the result, the installation manual and the adopted NEC.

Boxes And Blocking In The Right Place

Square, Secure, Aimed At The Seating

Heat aimed at the seating, from a heater that sits square and secure, is what your buyer feels on the first night.

  • Heater junction box at each heater location, per manual. Box type, offset and whether it is integral to the recess kit: To confirm
  • Wall mounts with the heater at least 72" above finished floor, and ceiling mounts at least 96"; clearance to the ceiling varies with output.3
  • Solid blocking between joists at every heater, pole and bracket, sized for heater and pole weight. Weights per model: To confirm
  • Suspension poles for high ceilings, with blocking and a box at the pole top.3
  • Clearances to combustibles, ceiling and sides, per manual. Do not interpolate between models.
  • Aim over the seating, not the floor centre, coordinated with the furniture plan.

Wiring The Controls

Warmth Exactly Where, And When, They Want It

Affinity Smart-Heat® Controls let your buyers enjoy warmth exactly where, and when, they want it, so rough them in together with the heater circuits. Specify the matching control for every heater, plus a wall switch.

Platinum, Tungsten electricAffinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller, with a wall switch.
EclipseEclipse controller, with a wall switch.
Wi-Fi and appBromic Smart-Heat app features require compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately.2
The Runs To Rough In
  • Line voltage: panel → Affinity controller → heaters, one controller channel per zone. Controller wiring topology: To confirm
  • Wall switch: controller → switch box at the threshold. Line- or low-voltage switch leg and box size: To confirm
  • Controller location: accessible, protected, within Wi-Fi range; check the signal there before close-in. Indoor or outdoor rating and mounting: To confirm
  • Integration: controller → home-automation panel for Control4, Crestron or Lutron; compatibility varies and may need extra hardware.2
  • Spare conduit: heater zone → controller location, so later zones or integration need no opened finishes.

Gas Stub-Outs For Gas Models

No Visible Lines On The Finished Patio

For buyers who want the presence of gas, a clean stub-out at framing keeps the finished patio free of visible lines.

  • A licensed gas fitter sizes, routes and tests the line to ANSI Z223.1/NFPA 546 and the local code.
  • Size for the connected load, using the total Btu/h of all appliances on the line, run length and supply pressure. Btu/h input, inlet size and pressure for Platinum Gas and Tungsten Gas built-in: To confirm
  • Stub out and cap at each heater location, with an accessible shut-off.
  • Allow for mount provisions. Heat deflectors reduce the clearance needed above gas heaters, and ceiling poles mount two heaters back to back.3 Leave room for both in the framing.
  • Confirm the fuel, natural gas or propane, before the heater is ordered.

Passing The Rough Inspection

Keep The Schedule Moving

A clean rough inspection keeps the schedule moving and the buyer's handover on time.

  • Permits pulled for electrical and gas work
  • Circuits match the approved plan and load calculation
  • Boxes accessible, supported and at the planned positions
  • Blocking in place for every heater and pole
  • Recess openings framed to the manual
  • Gas line pressure-tested and capped; shut-offs accessible
  • Installation manuals on site for the inspector
  • Final: heaters listed and marked (Intertek, UL or CSA);3 clearances met; controls working
General Guidance

This guide is general guidance, not an engineering or code determination. Confirm requirements with the local building authority. Electrical work by a licensed electrician to the adopted NFPA 70;4 gas work by a licensed gas fitter to ANSI Z223.1/NFPA 54;6 both to the manufacturer's installation instructions.
