01 · THE CODE

Getting The Code Right From The Start

A job that passes inspection the first time is the job that earns the next referral. Bromic electric heaters are installed to ANSI/NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code, as your state and city adopt it.4,11

GETTING IT RIGHT THE FIRST TIMEWHAT TRIPS UP AN OUTDOOR JOB

  1. Heaters fed from a shared circuit instead of their own, so the breaker trips on the first cold night.
  2. Cable runs left unprotected where a patio will see heavy use.
  3. Heaters mounted below the minimum height. Bromic's guidance is at least 72 in above the floor for a wall mount and at least 96 in for a ceiling mount; it varies with output, so take the figure from the installation manual for the model.4
  4. Supply voltage left unchecked against the heater. A 240 V heater on a 208 V supply gives less heat than the client was promised.
  5. Controls left until the end, with no box or cable run planned for them.

A job that misses the code costs you twice, first in red tags, stop-work orders and repeat visits, and then in the referral that never comes.

HOW WE HELP YOU GET IT RIGHT

  • A free heating design plan for every project, with heater selection and positions on the client's plan, coverage, and a parts and controls list you can price from.7,10
  • Installation manuals and CAD and Revit/BIM files for every heater, from the technical resources page.10
  • Technical support from people who know the products, on 1-800-301-1293, Monday to Friday, 9 am to 4 pm Pacific.7
  • The pre-wire guide (A16), covering circuits, boxes, blocking, recess openings and control runs for every heater type, with a hand-off sheet.

CODES VARY BY LOCATION

Confirm every installation with the local building authority and the authority having jurisdiction; electrical work must be carried out by a licensed electrician. The primary source is NFPA 70 as adopted locally.11

Tungsten Smart-Heat® Electric runs on 220–240 V and 208 V, and the 4000 W and 6000 W models also run on 277 V, the commercial supply many restaurants and hotels have.5 Commercial projects can choose 208 V, 240 V or 277 V supply.7 Match the heater to the supply at the design stage, well before the day of install.

02 · BEFORE ROUGH-IN  ·  03 · THE WEATHER

Built For The Weather, Season After Season

Outdoor equipment lives with rain, salt air, wind and the long swing from summer to winter. A heater that fails in its second season costs you a callback and a client, so we build ours to last.

YOUR CHECKLIST BEFORE ROUGH-IN

  1. 01Supply voltage confirmed for every heater (208, 240 or 277 V).
  2. 02One dedicated circuit per heater or per controller channel, sized from the installation manual and the code.
  3. 03Controller location chosen: dry, reachable, inside Wi-Fi range if the app is used.
  4. 04Box and cable run to every wall switch.
  5. 05Recess framing or suspension pole fixing agreed with the builder.4
  6. 06Integration with the home or building system confirmed with the integrator.

04 · THE QUOTE

Making Your Quote The One They Remember

Clients compare quotes, and most look alike, with a heater, a price and a date. With a little more care, yours stays in their mind.

1

Send a heating plan with the quote.

A Bromic design plan shows where each heater goes and the space it warms, turning a line item into a picture of the evening ahead.10

2

Quote the complete system.

Heaters, controllers, wall switch and app set-up belong in one price, because a heater is a finished job once its controls are in, and the client feels the difference on the first cold night.

3

Offer the pre-wire on every patio.

Rough-in at framing costs far less than opening a finished soffit later, and it lets the owner add heat after they move in.

4

Talk about safety plainly.

Dedicated circuits, the right mounting height and outdoor-rated controls build confidence, and clients trust the contractor who explains why.

5

Show it working.

A heated patio at dusk sells the next one. Ask to photograph the finished job; as a Bromic partner you can have projects featured.
