FROM OPTIONAL TO ESSENTIAL:
OUTDOOR HEATING APPLIANCES



   Power             System          Integration
   Planning          Planning        Systems


Outdoor heating appliances are increasingly
transitioning from portable accessories to built-in
infrastructure within modern homes.

This shift mirrors earlier trends in home design,
where appliances evolved from optional add-ons to
integrated features—such as built-in dishwashers in
the 1970s, built-in kitchens in the 2000s, and
built-in grills in the 2010s.

Now, in the 2020s, outdoor heating is following the
same trajectory, becoming a standard element in
outdoor living spaces.

Supporting this trend, infrared patio heaters have
seen a 39% increase in popularity among product
categories, according to the AIA Home Design
Trends Survey.

    PLAN FROM THE START TO
     ENJOY ALL SEASON LONG

Outdoor spaces are no longer optional—they
are extensions of the home. Designing them for
year-round use begins at the architectural
 stage, where key considerations like power,
layout, mounting, and integration are
addressed holistically.

When planned early, these elements work together,
resulting in better performance, a more seamless
design, and spaces that are consistently usable
across seasons. When introduced later, they often
become constraints that limit both effectiveness
and aesthetics.

WHAT EARLY PLANNING ENABLES

• Proper electrical and gas capacity

• Seamless integration with lighting and controls

• More refined, cohesive architectural design

• Extended use across a wider range of conditions

For builders and architects, this represents an
opportunity to elevate outdoor living into a fully
realized system—one in which heating is
considered alongside lighting, shade,
and ventilation.

For homeowners, it means creating spaces that are
not only visually appealing but consistently
comfortable and usable throughout the year.



 The most successful outdoor spaces
 are designed from the start—
 not added later.
