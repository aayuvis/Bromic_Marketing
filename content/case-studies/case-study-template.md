Use this for every new case study. Bromic already publishes 35 on bromic.com/case-studies/. The audit of those
(`data/site-inventory.json`, kind `case-study`) shows what to fix next time: 21 of 35 never name the controls, three heat-plan
images do not display, and few carry a number a reader can use.

## Structure (600–900 words, one page)

**Headline.** The venue or home, the place, the outcome in a few words. *"[Venue], [city]: [outcome]"*.

**At a glance** (a box, never prose)

| | |
|---|---|
| Project | [[VERIFY: name — regional lead]] |
| Location | City, state/country |
| Segment | Residential · restaurant · hotel · marine · workplace · multifamily · healthcare |
| Architect / designer / builder / installer | Names, with permission to credit |
| Products | Model, wattage or BTU, finish, quantity, mounting (recess kit, pole, bracket) |
| Controls | Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off, Dimmer or Eclipse controller, wall switch, Bromic Smart-Heat app, number of zones. **Never blank** |
| Heated area | ft² (m² second, for AU/EU editions) |
| Installed | Month and year |
| Photography | Photographer credit, with the licence for web, print and social |

**1. Situation.** The space before. Who uses it, when, and what didn't work (cold evenings, a short season, propane cylinders,
glare, noise, a ceiling too low for the obvious heater). Two short paragraphs.

**2. The brief.** What the owner, architect or operator asked for, in their words where permitted. The constraints: mounting
points, ceiling height, open perimeter (30% or more means outdoor), coastal exposure, power available, local code.

**3. The solution.** Which heaters, where, why. The controls and zones, and what each zone serves. The heat plan, as an image with
alt text and a caption. Installation notes a builder or electrician would want (circuits, recess kit, clearances). Every heater
recommendation names its controls; Wi-Fi and app features need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately.

**4. The result, with numbers.** Only figures the customer has approved in writing: extra covers, nights or months added, seats
heated, running cost, payback, energy versus the previous heaters. Say how each was measured and over what period. No number
without permission and a basis. A customer quote, attributed, approved.

**5. Photos.** Hero (the space in use, at dusk, heaters visible and correctly installed), a detail (the heater and its mounting),
the controller or app in use, the heat plan. Every photo: product, setting, photographer, licence, alt text. Add them to
`images/inbox/<project>/`.

**CTA.** One: book a design consult (A07). For an architect-led project, offer the spec kit (A11) instead.

## Interview guide (30 minutes; owner or operator, then the architect or installer)

1. What did the space look like before, and how often could you use it?
2. What made you decide to heat it, and when in the project did that come up?
3. Who specified the heaters, and what else did you consider?
4. What were the constraints: ceiling height, mounting, power, gas, coastal air, local rules?
5. How are the heaters controlled day to day, and who does it? How many zones, and why those?
6. What changed after the first season: nights, months, covers, bookings, how guests or family use it?
7. Can you share figures, and how were they measured? (Covers, revenue, running cost, nights open.)
8. What would you tell an architect, builder or operator about to do the same?
9. Anything you would do differently?
10. Who else should we speak to (architect, builder, electrician), and may we credit them?

## Consent checklist (all ticked before drafting is shared outside Bromic)

- [ ] Written permission to name the customer and the venue or home, with the exact wording to use
- [ ] Written approval of every number, with its basis, and of every quote, word for word
- [ ] Permission to credit the architect, designer, builder and installer, by name and spelling
- [ ] A photo licence covering web, print, social and paid use, and the photographer's credit line
- [ ] Residential: no house number, street or anything that locates the home without the owner's consent
- [ ] Final draft approved by the customer before publication; approval saved with the source files
