It depends on the model and its output. Clearance to combustibles is set by each heater's installation manual and its certification listing, so take the figures from the manual for the exact model and voltage on the drawings. There is no safe universal number. Bromic's general guidance gives minimum mounting heights of 72 in above floor for wall mounts and 96 in for ceiling mounts, and notes that ceiling clearance varies with energy output[^s1].

[[VERIFY: clearance-to-combustibles table (top, sides, front, below) per current model — Mike Clifford]]

| Clearance | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|
| Above the heater (ceiling, soffit, beam) | Installation manual, per model and output[^s1] |
| To side walls and returns | Installation manual |
| In front of the heater (people, fabric, furniture) | Installation manual |
| Mounting height above floor | Manual; Bromic minimums 72 in wall, 96 in ceiling[^s1] |
| Gas heaters | Manual and NFPA 54; heat deflectors can reduce top clearance[^s1] |

## What to draw

- [ ] The clearance envelope around each heater on the RCP and sections
- [ ] Timber soffits, beams, pergola slats and cladding inside or near the envelope
- [ ] Umbrellas, awnings, curtains, planting and signage that move into the envelope
- [ ] Mounting height dimensioned to finished floor

Specify each heater with its controls: an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller, a wall switch and the Bromic Smart-Heat app. Wi-Fi and app features require compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately[^s2].

*Codes cover where heaters may be installed and their clearance from combustibles[^s1]. Confirm with the installation manual, the authority having jurisdiction, and a licensed electrician or gas fitter.*
