# Outdoor Living Statistics 2027: How Europe Lives Outdoors

Three in four British adults have a private garden, and four in five people in the Netherlands live in a house.[^s1][^s2] Summer 2026 was the UK's hottest on record, and 2025 brought one of the sunniest years ever measured in the Netherlands and Germany.[^s3][^s4][^s5] Dutch cafés and restaurants grew their turnover, renewables supply nearly half of Europe's electricity, and UK garden centre sales of barbecues and heating rose 25% in July 2026.[^s6][^s10][^s13]

Every one of these numbers comes back to the same evening: a table laid on the terrace, friends who would happily stay another hour, and the cool air that eventually calls everyone in. Together they show how much of European life now happens under the open sky, from a London pub garden to a Brussels terrace.

![A covered Dutch terrace at dusk beside a glass-walled kitchen, heaters recessed in the ceiling](img:w-84-hr-grosveldvandervelde-nl1-2021)

## How Many British Adults Have A Garden?

77% of British adults have access to a private garden – about 44 million people.[^s1] For most households the garden is already part of the home, and the question is how many evenings of the year it can be used.

Source: Horticultural Trades Association, garden industry statistics, 2025.

## How Many Europeans Live In A House?

In the EU, 51.7% of people lived in a house in 2023. The Netherlands (79%) and Belgium (77%) are among the highest shares in Europe, and both figures include terraced houses with their own back gardens.[^s2]

Source: Eurostat, Housing in Europe, 2024 edition (2023 data).

## Where Do Germans Live Outdoors?

61% of people in Germany live in a flat, one of the highest shares in the EU.[^s2] For millions of households the balcony, the roof terrace and the courtyard are the outdoor room, and heat that mounts on a wall or a ceiling keeps that floor space free.

Source: Eurostat, Housing in Europe, 2024 edition (2023 data).

## How Warm Was The Summer Of 2026 In The UK?

The UK's mean summer temperature in 2026 was 16.5°C, provisionally the warmest summer since records began in 1884 and ahead of the 16.1°C set in 2025.[^s3] All five of the UK's warmest summers have come since 2003.[^s3] Warmer summers bring more evenings outside, and the cool hour after sunset is still when guests reach for a jacket.

Source: Met Office, summer 2026 provisional statistics, September 2026.

## How Sunny Was 2025 In The Netherlands?

About 2,124 hours of sunshine made 2025 the second sunniest year in the Netherlands since observations began, against a normal of 1,774 hours.[^s4] The year averaged 11.4°C.[^s4]

Source: KNMI, year overview 2025.

## How Sunny Was 2025 In Germany?

Germany had more than 1,945 hours of sunshine in 2025, about 17% more than the 1991–2020 average and one of the five sunniest years since 1951.[^s5]

Source: Deutscher Wetterdienst, the weather in Germany in 2025, December 2025.

## Are Dutch Cafés And Restaurants Growing?

They are. Turnover at Dutch eating and drinking establishments rose 4.8% in 2025, and hospitality as a whole grew 3.9% on 2024.[^s6]

Source: CBS (Statistics Netherlands), hospitality turnover, March 2026.

## What Does A Warm Spell Do For A British Pub?

It fills the garden. Britain's managed pub groups grew like-for-like sales by 9.1% in April 2025, as warmer weather drew people into beer gardens and terraces.[^s7] A garden that is covered and warm keeps that trade on cooler evenings too.

Source: CGA RSM Hospitality Business Tracker, April 2025 (reported by The Morning Advertiser).

## Are Pavement Terraces In England Here To Stay?

Yes. Pavement licences became permanent in England on 31 March 2024. Councils can grant them for up to two years, and heaters are named among the furniture a licence covers, alongside umbrellas and barriers.[^s8] Conditions vary by council, so check the local licence before choosing a heater.

Source: Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, pavement licences guidance, 2024.

## Can A Terrace Be Heated In France?

On the public domain, only when it is enclosed. Since 31 March 2022, French law has banned heating or air-conditioning café, bar and restaurant terraces on public land, whatever the heater. Terraces that are covered and closed on their sides by solid walls are exempt unless the local authority objects, and private land such as an interior courtyard is not covered.[^s9]

Source: Décret n° 2022-452 du 30 mars 2022, as summarised by CCI Paris Île-de-France.

## How Clean Is Europe's Electricity?

In 2024, 47.5% of the electricity consumed in the EU came from renewable sources. The share was 50.5% in the Netherlands and 54.1% in Germany.[^s10]

Source: Eurostat, renewable electricity, January 2026.

## How Much UK Electricity Comes From Renewables?

Renewables supplied 47% of UK electricity in 2025, a record high.[^s11] More of the power behind an electric heater now comes from wind and sun.

Source: Carbon Brief, UK electricity in 2025, January 2026.

## What Will Heat New Homes In England?

Low-carbon heating. The Future Homes and Buildings Standards come into force on 24 March 2027, subject to transitional provisions, so that new homes are built with low-carbon heating and high levels of energy efficiency.[^s12] For a new home, that makes electric outdoor heat the natural partner, drawn into the plan from the start.

Source: Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, Future Homes and Buildings Standards: consultation response, March 2026.

## How Much Do UK Households Spend On The Garden?

UK households spent around £9 billion on retail garden products in 2025.[^s1]

Source: Horticultural Trades Association, garden industry statistics, 2025.

## Are Barbecues And Heating Selling In The UK?

Strongly. UK garden centre sales of barbecues and heating rose 25% in July 2026 against July 2025, while garden centre sales as a whole rose 11%.[^s13] Shoppers are buying for the evening as well as the afternoon.

Source: Horticultural Trades Association Market Update, July 2026 (reported by Insight DIY, August 2026).

## What These Numbers Tell Us

- Most households in the UK, the Netherlands and Belgium have a garden or a house with outdoor space, and in Germany the balcony and terrace do the same job.[^s1][^s2]
- Brighter, warmer years bring more evenings outside, and the cool hour after sunset decides how long they last.[^s3][^s4][^s5]
- Terraces and pub gardens earn when they are full, and England has made pavement terraces permanent.[^s6][^s7][^s8]
- France allows heat on public terraces only when they are enclosed, England names heaters in its pavement licences, and new English homes will be built with low-carbon heating, while renewables supply a record share of the power.[^s8][^s9][^s11][^s12]
- How a terrace feels after dark and through spring and autumn is decided at the planning stage, when power, mounting and controls can be drawn into the design from the very beginning.

## FAQ

### How many people in the UK have a garden?

77% of British adults have access to a private garden, about 44 million people, according to the Horticultural Trades Association (2025).[^s1]

### What share of Europeans live in a house rather than a flat?

In the EU, 51.7% of people lived in a house in 2023. The share is 79% in the Netherlands and 77% in Belgium, while 61% of people in Germany live in a flat.[^s2]

### Was summer 2026 the hottest on record in the UK?

Provisionally, yes. The UK's mean summer temperature was 16.5°C, the highest since records began in 1884, according to the Met Office.[^s3]

### Can pubs and restaurants in England put heaters on a pavement terrace?

Heaters are named among the furniture a pavement licence covers, and licences have been permanent in England since 31 March 2024. Each council sets its own conditions, so check the local licence.[^s8]

### Are terrace heaters banned in France?

Heating open terraces on public land has been banned in France since 31 March 2022, whatever the heater. Covered terraces closed on their sides by solid walls are exempt unless the local authority objects, and private land is not covered.[^s9]
