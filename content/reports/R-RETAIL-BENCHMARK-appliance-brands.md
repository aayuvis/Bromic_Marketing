## Executive summary

- **The strongest dealer programs are tiered and earned.** Infratech's Elite Retailer gets a fourth warranty year (after its Certified Installer program), priority on local leads, a free 24-inch looping-video display, POP and monthly sales training.[^s1][^s2] Traeger (Platinum, Gold, Silver) and Weber (certified and Experience Center stores) also tier their dealers in public.
- **Premium kitchen brands win the specifier, then send the client to a dealer.** Sub-Zero, Thermador, Fisher & Paykel and Miele all have a public trade hub: spec library, CAD/BIM, a planning guide, CEU courses, experience centres and named trade reps.[^s5][^s6][^s7] Sub-Zero's "Living Kitchen Experience" labels its best dealer showrooms.
- **Self-serve tools come up again and again.** Sub-Zero's Brochure Maker and Reveal spec tool, Thermador's Text 4 Specs (text a model number to 21432), EcoSmart Fire's B2B portal with CAD, AR/3D models and live inventory.[^s4][^s5][^s6]
- **Grill brands sell through experience.** Weber runs courses at certified retailers and has trained associates on live grills from mobile trucks. Big Green Egg dealers must run demonstrator EGGs and host EGGfests.[^s9][^s10]
- **What nobody shows publicly:** e-commerce content kits (retailer product pages, A+ modules), co-op terms and display-fixture catalogs. These sit behind dealer logins. Bromic's equivalent should be built for the portal, not the open web.

## Five standouts worth copying

1. **Infratech Elite Retailer (the direct competitor).** One page lists what a dealer earns: an asset library with style guides and images, POP, a free 24-inch TV display running a branded loop, monthly training for sales reps, priority on leads and a fourth warranty year after its Certified Installer program.[^s1][^s2] It works because every benefit is something the dealer can sell with, and the warranty year ties dealer training to a consumer promise. Its installer gets a branded Milwaukee Packout tray of parts and tools: a small, physical reason to stay loyal.
2. **Sub-Zero Brochure Maker and Reveal.** A designer or dealer builds a client-ready brochure or spec set from the models chosen, then saves, shares and edits it.[^s5] It works because it turns the brand's content into the dealer's own proposal, with the brand's accuracy.
3. **Thermador Text 4 Specs.** Text a model number to 21432 and the specs arrive on your phone.[^s6] On a showroom floor or a job site, that is the fastest answer a salesperson can give.
4. **Weber's retail experience.** Courses held at certified retailers, a tiered store finder (Weber Store, Experience Center, certified store), and associates trained on live grills from mobile trucks.[^s9] It works because the store becomes where you learn, not only where you buy. Bromic's version is a heat demonstration night.
5. **EcoSmart Fire / HEATSCOPE B2B portal.** One login for specs, CAD, AR/3D models, live inventory and ordering, with tiered discounts and 10% credit back on purchases.[^s4] It works because ordering and selling content share one place, and AR lets a client see a heater on their own patio.

## Implications for Bromic

Bromic's selling content is already broader than most benchmarks show in public: twelve retail one-pagers, three calculators, a planning guide, an architect spec kit, a builder kit and an electrical contractor guide. Bromic also runs a public dealer training schedule (heating.bromic.com/training-schedule), and bromic.com has a retail-partner page that could not be read here (Cloudflare). What is missing is the **program and the plumbing around the content**:

1. **A tiered retail partner program with published benefits.** Infratech, Traeger and Weber name their tiers. Bromic's Partner Program is written for installers and builders; retailers need their own, with earned benefits (leads, display support, training status) [[VERIFY: what the retail-partner page already offers — Trade sales]].
2. **A self-serve asset library for dealers.** Logos, style guide, approved product and lifestyle images, video loops, one-pagers in print and digital form, behind one login. Only Infratech shows one publicly; it is the cheapest gap to close.
3. **An e-commerce content kit for national retailers.** Product titles, bullets, spec tables, image sets to each retailer's standard, video, comparison charts and A+ / enhanced content modules for Best Buy, Costco, Ferguson and others. No benchmark shows this publicly, but every national account will ask for it.
4. **A display and demonstration program.** A live heater demo bay specification, a looping video screen, shelf talkers and swatch boards for colours and fascias. Bromic's merchandising one-pager tells a store what to do; Infratech and Big Green Egg supply or require the hardware.
5. **Floor-staff certification.** On-demand modules and a short test, with a "Bromic certified" status that unlocks benefits (Infratech ties a warranty year to it). The training schedule is live; a certificate and its rewards are not seen.
6. **A self-serve spec and proposal tool.** A brochure or quote builder from chosen models and controls, and a Text-4-Specs style lookup for the floor, building on the sizing calculator.
7. **Lead referral and a complimentary layout service.** Route web enquiries to certified dealers, and offer a free heater layout from plans (Infratech, Fisher & Paykel, Kalamazoo do this for the trade).
8. **CEU course and BIM/Revit families.** Sub-Zero and Infratech give AIA/ASID credit; Fisher & Paykel ships BIM with embedded metadata. The architect spec kit would sit inside a CEU.
9. **Co-op and promotion toolkit.** Co-branded ad templates, seasonal campaign kits (autumn heating season) and clear co-op rules.
10. **Consumer event kit and project showcase.** A "warm nights" demo-evening kit for dealers, and a dealer and designer project contest that feeds the case-study library.
