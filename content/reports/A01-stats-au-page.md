# Outdoor Living Statistics 2027: How Australia Lives Outdoors

"Outdoor area" was the fifth most-searched home feature on realestate.com.au in 2025, and "heating" was tenth.[^s1] Australians approved 205,249 new dwellings in 2025-26[^s3] and spent A$56.2 billion renovating the homes they have.[^s4] Winters are milder, yet Melbourne still fell to 3.2 °C in 2026,[^s6] and outdoor dining is now permanent policy in Sydney.[^s8]

Every one of these numbers comes back to the same evening: a table set under the pergola, friends who would happily stay another hour, and the chill that settles in after sunset. Together they show how much of Australian life is lived outside.

![A covered harbourside lounge in Sydney under a vaulted ceiling, four white heaters on angled stems](img:w-residential-australia-sydney-03)

## What Do Australian Home Buyers Search For?

Outdoor space ranks high. "Outdoor area" was the fifth most-searched keyword on realestate.com.au in 2025, "balcony" was seventh and "heating" tenth; only "swimming pool", "garage", "air conditioning" and "ensuite" ranked above the outdoor area.[^s1] In Canberra, searches for "courtyard" grew 62% over the past twelve months.[^s1]

Source: PropTrack, Rising keyword searches reveal emerging home buying trends, January 2026.

## How Many New Homes Is Australia Approving?

205,249 dwellings were approved in 2025-26, a 9.2% increase on the year before.[^s3] Apartments led the growth: 48,778 apartment dwellings were approved, up 13.2%.[^s3] More apartments mean more balconies and rooftops to plan as outdoor rooms.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, Building Approvals, Australia, June 2026 release (original terms).

## How Big Is A Typical Australian Block?

The median land area of a house sold in Australia in 2025 was 645 m², and the average newly built house has 241 m² of floor area.[^s2] On a compact block, every square metre outside has to work hard, and a well-planned alfresco becomes a room of its own.

Source: PropTrack, land and house size analysis, October 2025.

## How Much Do Australians Spend On Renovation?

A$56.2 billion in 2025-26, up almost 5% in a year and 37% of all housing spending.[^s4] Australians are investing in the homes they already have, and the existing alfresco is often the next room to finish.

Source: KPMG Australia, New builds and renovation spending surge as knock-down rebuilds decline, October 2026.

## How Cold Do Australian Winter Nights Get?

Cold enough to call everyone inside. Melbourne's coldest night of 2026 fell to 3.2 °C on 6 July, and Hobart dropped to 2.6 °C the same night.[^s6] The winter itself was the warmest on record in three capitals, with provisional means of 15.29 °C in Sydney, 12.18 °C in Melbourne and 8.14 °C in Canberra.[^s5] Milder days bring more winter evenings worth spending outside, once the chill after sunset is handled.

Source: Weatherzone, 6 July 2026 and 31 August 2026 (provisional seasonal averages).

## How Big Is Australian Hospitality?

Australia's cafés, restaurants and takeaway food services turned over A$66.273 billion in the financial year to June 2025, up 2.5% on the year before.[^s7]

Source: Restaurant & Catering Australia, from ABS Retail Trade data, August 2025.

## Is Outdoor Dining Here To Stay In Sydney?

It is. The City of Sydney has approved nearly 1,000 outdoor dining applications since November 2020, supporting more than 800 businesses and about 13,000 m² of outdoor dining area, and waived outdoor dining fees permanently from 1 July 2025.[^s8] More than a third of participating businesses saw a 20% increase in turnover.[^s8]

Source: City of Sydney, Outdoor dining fees permanently waived across Sydney, 1 July 2025.

![A busy wharf café terrace in Sydney, heaters on the slatted ceiling above the diners](img:w-commercial-australia-sydney-manly-wharf-15)

## Are New Australian Homes Going All-Electric?

In Victoria and the ACT, yes. From 1 January 2027, all new homes in Victoria must be built all-electric, as must new commercial buildings other than industrial, manufacturing and agricultural ones.[^s9] The ACT has prevented new gas network connections for residential buildings and commercial and community facility zones since 8 December 2023; existing connections are not affected.[^s10]

Source: Premier of Victoria, 24 June 2025; ACT Government, 2023.

## How Many Australian Homes Make Their Own Power?

About 40% of Australian dwellings had rooftop solar in April 2025.[^s11] Rooftop solar reached 28.3 GW across about 4.3 million installations by the end of 2025, and 454,753 home batteries had been installed, 183,245 of them in the second half of 2025 alone, about four times the same period of 2024.[^s12][^s13]

Source: Australian Energy Council, June 2025; Clean Energy Council, Rooftop Solar and Storage Report, July to December 2025.

## How Connected Are Australian Homes?

86% of Australian households owned at least one smart home product in June 2026, up 10 percentage points since 2024.[^s14] Households with a smart home budget rose from 44% to 71% in two years, and the average budget passed A$3,500 in 2026.[^s14]

Source: Telsyte, Australian Smart Home Market Study 2025–2029, as reported by iTWire, October 2026.

## What These Numbers Tell Us

- Australians search for outdoor space and build it on compact blocks and balconies.[^s1][^s2][^s3]
- Renovation spending is close to its peak, so the existing alfresco is the next room to finish.[^s4]
- Winters are milder, yet the nights still fall cold, and outdoor dining is here to stay.[^s5][^s6][^s8]
- New homes are going all-electric, so outdoor heat, power and controls belong in the first drawing.[^s9][^s10]
