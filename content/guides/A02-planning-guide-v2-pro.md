# Plan The Outdoor Heat: Professional Edition

For architects, designers, builders and electricians: six decisions that put the heat on the drawings, from the zones and the mounts to the circuits and the controls, before the ceiling closes. Drawn in from schematic design, the heat is built exactly as specified.

![Minimal glass pavilion at dusk, heaters recessed flush into the ceiling](img:l-010-075)

## Draw The Heat In From Schematic Design

When heat arrives late, it tends to arrive as surface conduit, a ceiling that no longer matches and a substitution at bid. Fewer than one in four homeowners prioritize heating at planning[^s1], so the brief usually starts with you. Six decisions, taken in order, give the drawing set everything it needs.

| | Decision | Output for the drawings |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Where do people sit? | Heated zones over seating and bar rails, ft² per zone, drawn on the RCP |
| 02 | How open is it? | Configuration and permanent open perimeter, in % |
| 03 | What can the heat hang from? | Mount per surface, finished heights AFF, recess details |
| 04 | How cold are the evenings? | Cover and climate factors stated; mounting height by use |
| 05 | Which heater, how many, which controls? | Model and count per zone, controller and channels, wall switch, app or BMS |
| 06 | Is the power in before the ceiling closes? | Amps per heater, circuits at 125%, gas load, rough-in, code path |

## Question 01. Map The Heat Before The Ceiling Fills Up

Mark where people sit, dine, stand and pass, then lay the heat over those zones before you choose a finish or a mount[^s25]. Heaters compete for the soffit with downlights, fans, speakers and ducts, so coordinate them with your MEP and lighting consultants in design development, before the reflected ceiling plan fills up[^s25].

**On the reflected ceiling plan**

- Heaters over the seating, in a row
- Downlights between the heaters, never under them
- Fans clear of the heat path
- Speakers and sensors off the heater line
- Junction boxes with access
- Heat zones drawn before finishes

**Three working rules**

- **About 20 ft² a seat.** Measure the seating and the bar rails, and leave out the circulation.
- **Fewer, higher-output heaters.** Fewer heaters placed where they work read as a calmer ceiling[^s25].
- **Match the finish.** Black, white, stainless, and graphite in the Select collection, so the heater belongs to the architecture[^s25][^s2].

![A recessed heater beside a downlight in a timber soffit](img:w-bromic-shoot-may-11-2023-54)

## Question 02. Confirm The Space Is Outdoors

For heating design, a space is outdoor when at least 30% of its horizontal perimeter is permanently open. A cover holds a gentle heat cloud, and sizing always rests on radiant coverage[^s3].

| Configuration | Qualifies | Design note |
|---|---|---|
| Open to the sky: walls round, one permanent ground-level opening | Yes, if the opening is 30% or more of the perimeter | No heat cloud; size for the exposure |
| Covered, two sides open | Yes | Heat cloud under the cover |
| Covered, three walls, 30% or more open | Yes | Strong heat cloud; check clearances |
| Fully enclosed, or glazing and screens that close below 30% | Check | Treat as enclosed; confirm with the AHJ and Bromic |

Rules per the 2021 guide[^s3].

## Question 03. Mount By Surface

What is overhead or behind the seats decides the mount. Real installations keep heaters near an edge, a wall, a beam or the open side, with the face toward the seats. Tungsten Smart-Heat Electric Series II mounts on a single post, with adaptors for pitched ceilings and for reaching out from a wall[^s29].

| Surface | Series II mount | Height |
|---|---|---|
| A wall or beam | Angled bracket on the single post | Wall 72 in or more[^s4] |
| A flat ceiling, near the edge | Short post, tilted toward the seats: up to 45° on 2 and 3 kW, 30° on 4 and 6 kW when ceiling-mounted[^s29] | Ceiling 96 in or more[^s4] |
| A pitched ceiling | Pitched-ceiling adaptor for 3/12–12/12; the post hangs vertical and the heater level[^s29] | Ceiling 96 in or more[^s4] |
| Out from the wall | Wall adaptor 14 in out with a 5 in drop, or 18 in out with a 12 in drop, face down[^s29] | Over a bar rail or banquette |
| Recessed | Low-clearance recess kit, 7.5 in cavity[^s30] | 94 in or more[^s30] |

The standard bracket is 5 in for 2 and 3 kW and 9.5 in for 4 and 6 kW; the 4 and 6 kW models are never ceiling-mounted on the 5 in post. Accessory poles of 600 and 1,200 mm bring the heater down from a high ceiling[^s29]. ## Question 04. State The Cover And The Climate

The same heater covers less in colder air, and wind draws heat out of the zone[^s25]. The sizing model states its factors, so every count is traceable.

- **Cover:** open 0.85 · partly covered 1.0 · covered 1.1
- **Climate:** mild 1.0 · cool 0.85 · cold 0.7
- **Freestanding:** never more than four seats each

**Heaters for the same 12 seats (240 ft²), Tungsten Smart-Heat Electric 4000 W**

| | Open | Partly covered | Covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mild | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| Cool | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| Cold | 4 | 3 | 3 |

On an exposed site, add glass screens or windbreaks on the windward side as part of the heating design[^s25]. The factors are planning assumptions in the Bromic sizing calculator, confirmed by the design team for each project, and are not published ratings. ### Set Heights By How The Zone Is Used

Radiant intensity falls with the square of distance[^s25]. Mount too high and the output is lost before it arrives; too low and the seat beneath runs hot.

| Zone use | Mounting height (Bromic guidance) | To a guest's head |
|---|---|---|
| Seated tables | About 8 ft | About 4 ft |
| High-tops and bar rails | About 10 ft | About 4 ft |
| Standing areas and entries | About 12 ft | About 6 ft |

**Twice as far, a quarter of the warmth.** For an ideal point source, a guest 1.25 times as far away receives about 64% of the warmth, 1.5 times as far about 44%, and twice as far about 25%[^s25]. Linear heaters fall off less close in.

A wall heater projects its warmth outward, so a table pushed against the wall beneath it sits in a dead zone. Set the table forward, and warm wide tables from both sides[^s25].

Heights by use are Bromic guidance[^s25], never below each model's minimum. [[VERIFY: mounting heights by use against each model's installation manual — Product team]]

## Every Outdoor Scheme Rests On Radiant Warmth

Convected heat rises and disperses, and forced air cannot hold an open space at temperature, so outdoor heat cannot be run from a thermostat[^s3]. Directional infrared aimed at people is what works.

| Mode | How it moves heat | Outdoors |
|---|---|---|
| Convection | Warms the air, which rises and drifts away | Works only where air is contained |
| Conduction | Through direct contact, surface to surface | Warms only what a guest touches |
| Radiant (infrared) | Waves absorbed by people and surfaces | Warms people directly and holds up in a light breeze |

Heat transfer: Bromic guidance[^s25]. An indirect heater, such as a mushroom heater, radiates in every direction and loses much of its output upward; a directional heater sends most of its output toward the occupied zone[^s25][^s3].

| | Short wave | Medium wave | Long wave |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infrared band | IR-A, 0.78–1.4 µm[^s27] | IR-B, 1.4–3 µm[^s27] | IR-C, 3 µm and beyond[^s27] |
| Typical element | About 2,400 K, peak about 1.2 µm[^s26] | About 1,200 K, peak about 2.4 µm[^s26] | About 700 K, peak about 4.1 µm[^s26] |
| Glow | The hottest elements; bright glow and glare | Soft glow, little glare | No visible glow |
| Warm-up | Instant[^s3] | About 30 seconds[^s3] | About 5 minutes; more easily dispersed by moving air[^s3] |
| Bromic | – | **Platinum, Eclipse and Tungsten Electric** | – |

All infrared travels in straight lines at the speed of light; reach depends on output, reflector and mounting height, and the band does not change it. Peak wavelengths follow Wien's law, λ ≈ 2,898 µm·K ÷ T[^s26], at typical element temperatures, for illustration. ### Count Per Zone, Factors Stated

- **Zone area ÷ effective coverage, rounded up**, where effective coverage = nominal coverage × cover × climate, from the sizing calculator.
- **Freestanding: the larger of that and seats ÷ 4**, because seated guests block the radiant heat of a freestanding unit; allow 20 ft² a seat where the seat count is unknown.
- **Zones meet and slightly overlap**, so there are no cold seams, and long tables are warmed from both sides.

## Confirm Clearances And Code Early

Each model is read on its own manual and listing, never interpolated from another. A heater outside its clearances can underheat the zone as well as breach the listing[^s25].

**Series II clearances**[^s29]: 12 in from a side wall and 9 in below the ceiling for 2 and 3 kW; 18 in and 13 in for 4 and 6 kW; at least 20 in between heaters; 36 in to combustibles in front of the heater.

| Family | Minimum height above the floor |
|---|---|
| Tungsten and Platinum Electric | Wall 72 in; ceiling 96 in[^s4][^s5][^s6] |
| Cobalt Electric | Wall 6 ft; ceiling 8 ft[^s9] |
| Eclipse Electric | 96 in[^s7] |
| Eclipse Freestanding | 79 in clearance[^s8] |
| Built-in gas (Platinum Gas, Tungsten Gas) | 96 in[^s10][^s11] |

A heat deflector reduces the clearance a built-in gas heater needs above it; it is recommended when the space above is under 1,100 mm (Platinum) or 42 in (Tungsten), or the unit is exposed[^s10][^s11].

| Gas | ANSI Z223.1/NFPA 54[^s22] | Location, supply, ventilation, clearance to combustibles |
| Certification | Intertek, UL or CSA mark | On every gas and electrical appliance, portable heaters included[^s3] |
