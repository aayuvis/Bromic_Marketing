Design the outdoor living area as a room: size it for what will happen there, give it cover, and turn it to the view and the sun. That is the method of Yvonne Haber, an Australian residential architect who has run her own practice since 1994 and tutors at UNSW[^s1]. She also treats heat as a standard inclusion, not an extra, because it is what keeps the room in use after dark and out of season.

![Rear of a renovated house with open sliders and a black Tungsten Electric heater on the terrace](img:w-yvone-haber-bronte-house-1)

## Who is Yvonne Haber?

Haber designs family homes: units, terraces, semi-detached and freestanding houses. Her work centres on open plans, natural light, cross ventilation and storage. She starts with how a household lives, including habits clients may not notice in themselves, and draws the spatial relationships from that[^s1].

Her practice is in Australia, but the brief she describes is the one US architects now hear. Outdoor areas are used as living rooms, by day and late into the evening[^s1].

## What are her three tests for an outdoor living area?

| Haber's test | What it means on a US project |
|---|---|
| **Proportion for the activity** | Size from the furniture out: clear space round the dining table, room for a lounge group. |
| **The option of cover** | A roof, pergola or cantilever over part of the space. It also gives heaters a mounting plane. |
| **Orientation and outlook** | Turn the room to the sun, the view and away from the prevailing wind. |

Her standard inclusions follow: a hard surface or deck for furniture, some form of outdoor heating, and soft landscaping for the outlook[^s1].

## What are US homeowners building outside?

The same rooms. In the 2026 U.S. Houzz Outdoor Trends Study, 83% of renovated outdoor spaces include a lounge or seating area[^s2]. Dining areas appear in 55% and outdoor cooking in 48%[^s3].

One figure tells an architect where heating stands. Of lounge areas, 48% include a fireplace or fire pit and 66% include lighting[^s3]. A fire pit sets the mood, but it warms only the people closest to it. Radiant heat over the seating keeps the whole group comfortable.

## Where does heat go in the design?

On the drawings, with the lights. In one of Haber's homes a Tungsten Smart-Heat Electric heater sits on the terrace[^s1]. The current Tungsten Electric range runs from 2,000 to 6,000 W and covers approx. 65–160 ft² depending on model and installation[^s4].

Specify the controls with it: an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller, a wall switch, and the Bromic Smart-Heat app for zones and schedules. Wi-Fi and app features require compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately[^s5]. Decide the circuit and the switch position at design stage, so nothing is surface-run later.

## Frequently asked

### What makes an outdoor living area get used?
Proportions that fit the furniture, cover over at least part of the space, an orientation that suits the sun and view, and heat for evenings and cooler months.

### Is a fire pit enough to heat an outdoor room?
A fire pit adds atmosphere but warms only the people near it. Radiant heaters mounted over the seating warm the whole group.

### When should heating be specified?
At design stage, with lighting and structure, so the circuit, mounting and controls are on the drawings.
