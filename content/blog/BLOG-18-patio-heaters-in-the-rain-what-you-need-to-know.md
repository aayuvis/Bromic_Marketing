Yes, within limits. A heater made for outdoor use is built to handle rain to the level of its IP (ingress protection) rating. IPX4 means it resists water splashed from any direction; IPX5 means it resists water jets[^s1]. Neither makes a heater something to leave exposed to every storm. Place it under cover where you can, choose the rating for the site, and follow the manual's care advice.

## What does an IP rating mean?

An IP code is defined by the international standard IEC 60529. The first digit rates protection against solids and dust, the second against water; an X means that part was not rated[^s2]. Test laboratories test products against it[^s1]. The IEC writes the standard; it does not accredit or certify individual products, as the earlier version of this post said. The codes are explained in full in [what is an IP rating](https://www.bromic.com/blog/what-is-an-ip-rating-and-why-is-it-important/).

| Rating | What it means | Typical exposure |
|---|---|---|
| IPX4 | Protected against splashing water from all directions[^s1] | Rain on a covered or semi-covered patio |
| IPX5 | Protected against water jets[^s1] | More exposed sites, coastal and marine settings |
| IP54 | Dust-protected and protected against splashing water[^s1] | Outdoor-mounted controls |

Safety certification is a separate thing. Bromic's electric heaters carry Intertek ETL/cETL listings, which concern electrical safety for the North American market[^s3]. [[VERIFY: whether Intertek also tested the IP ratings, and the test house for each — Mike Clifford]]

## How do you protect a heater from rain?

**Mount it under cover.** A soffit, roof or deep eave keeps most rain off and also holds heat. Recessed and ceiling-mounted electric heaters sit naturally out of the weather.

**Match the rating to the exposure.** Proximity to water is one of the first site factors Bromic's planning guide lists[^s5]. On a coast, a pool edge or a boat, choose the Marine model; see [coastal outdoor heaters](https://www.bromic.com/blog/stylish-resilient-coastal-outdoor-heaters/).

**Have it wired properly.** Electric heaters are hard-wired to the electrical code in force and gas heaters connected to the fuel gas code[^s5]. Use a licensed electrician or gas fitter and confirm the requirements with your local building authority. Outdoor wiring questions are covered in [does an outdoor electric heater need GFCI protection](item:BLD-BY-KB1).

**Care for it.** Keep it clean and free of debris, check it after storms, and follow the manual on covers and seasonal storage, especially for freestanding units. More in [cleaning and maintenance for outdoor heaters](https://www.bromic.com/blog/keeping-the-heat-on-essential-cleaning-and-maintenance-for-outdoor-heaters/).

## Frequently asked

### Can I use my patio heater while it is raining?
An outdoor-rated heater can run in rain up to its IP rating[^s1]. Check the rating and the manual for your model; if a heater has been flooded or damaged, have it checked before use.

### Are Bromic heaters waterproof?
"Waterproof" is not an IP term. Bromic publishes IPX4 for Tungsten Electric and IPX5 for Platinum Electric Marine[^s3]; other models are listed above.

### What is the difference between IPX4 and IPX5?
IPX4 is tested against splashing water from all directions; IPX5 against water jets[^s1].

### Is it safe to leave a patio heater on overnight?
Do not leave a heater running unattended. Use Affinity schedules and timers to switch heat off when the space is empty.
