Yes, within its limits. A heater made for outdoor use is built to handle rain to the level of its IP (ingress protection) rating, where IPX4 means it resists water splashed from any direction and IPX5 means it resists water jets[^s1]. Even so, no heater is meant to stand exposed to every storm. Place yours under cover wherever you can, choose the rating your site calls for and follow the manual's care advice, and it will keep the evening warm long after the first drops fall.

## Understanding The IP Rating

An IP code is defined by the international standard IEC 60529. The first digit rates protection against solids and dust and the second against water, with an X meaning that part was not rated[^s2]. Test laboratories test products against it[^s1]. The IEC writes the standard and does not accredit or certify individual products, as the earlier version of this post suggested. The codes are explained in full in [what is an IP rating](https://www.bromic.com/blog/what-is-an-ip-rating-and-why-is-it-important/).

| Rating | What it means | Typical exposure |
|---|---|---|
| IPX4 | Protected against splashing water from all directions[^s1] | Rain on a covered or semi-covered patio |
| IPX5 | Protected against water jets[^s1] | More exposed sites, coastal and marine settings |
| IP54 | Dust-protected and protected against splashing water[^s1] | Outdoor-mounted controls |

Safety certification is a separate matter. Bromic's electric heaters carry Intertek ETL/cETL listings, which cover electrical safety for the North American market[^s3]. [[VERIFY: whether Intertek also tested the IP ratings, and the test house for each — Product team]]

## Caring For A Heater That Lives Outdoors

**Mount it under cover.** A soffit, roof or deep eave keeps most of the rain off and holds warmth in as well. Recessed and ceiling-mounted electric heaters sit naturally out of the weather.

**Match the rating to the exposure.** Proximity to water is one of the first site factors Bromic's planning guide lists[^s5]. On a coast, at a pool edge or aboard a boat, choose the Marine model; see [coastal outdoor heaters](https://www.bromic.com/blog/stylish-resilient-coastal-outdoor-heaters/).

**Have it installed properly.** Electric heaters are hard-wired to the electrical code in force, and gas heaters are connected to the fuel gas code[^s5]. Use a licensed electrician or gas fitter and confirm the requirements with your local building authority. Outdoor wiring questions are covered in [does an outdoor electric heater need GFCI protection](item:BLD-BY-KB1).

**Look after it.** Keep it clean and free of debris, check it after storms, and follow the manual on covers and seasonal storage, especially for freestanding units. Read more in [cleaning and maintenance for outdoor heaters](https://www.bromic.com/blog/keeping-the-heat-on-essential-cleaning-and-maintenance-for-outdoor-heaters/).

## Frequently Asked

### Can I use my patio heater while it is raining?
An outdoor-rated heater can run in the rain up to its IP rating[^s1]. Check the rating and the manual for your model, and if a heater has been flooded or damaged, have it checked before use.

### Are Bromic heaters waterproof?
"Waterproof" is not an IP term. Bromic publishes IPX4 for Tungsten Electric and IPX5 for Platinum Electric Marine[^s3], and the other models are listed above.

### What is the difference between IPX4 and IPX5?
IPX4 is tested against splashing water from all directions, and IPX5 against water jets[^s1].

### Is it safe to leave a patio heater on overnight?
Never leave a heater running unattended. Affinity schedules and timers can switch the warmth off once the space is empty.
