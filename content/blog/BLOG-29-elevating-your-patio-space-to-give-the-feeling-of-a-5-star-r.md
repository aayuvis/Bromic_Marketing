A patio feels like a resort when it is warm where you sit, softly lit after dark, furnished to last and built around one thing worth looking at. Start with heat you can feel, not see: radiant warmth over the lounge or dining table, so the evening does not end when the sun goes down. Then layer light, choose weatherproof materials, give the space a focal point and set it all from one control.

![Arched freestanding Eclipse heater over a lounge on a covered terrace opening to the kitchen](img:w-bromic-eclipse-heater-scottsdale-home-6)

## 1. Put warmth where people sit

Resorts heat the seat, not the sky. Radiant heat warms people and objects rather than air, so it works outdoors; under a partial cover it also builds a little pocket of warmth.[^s5] Place heat directly over the table or sofa, not at the edge of the space.

Where there is no wall or ceiling to mount on, the Eclipse Smart-Heat Freestanding Electric reaches over a table on a curved arm from a weighted, wheeled base, covering about 121 ft².[^s1] Where there is a soffit, a fixed heater on a pole or in the ceiling keeps the floor clear. Bromic's [complimentary design service](https://www.bromic.com/design-your-space/) works out how many heaters and where.

The Eclipse was featured by The Wall Street Journal in a piece on turning a patio into a resort.[^s3] [[VERIFY: WSJ article date, headline and what it says about Eclipse — Megan Spies]]

## 2. Layer the light

Use three kinds: ambient light to see by, task light where you cook or pour, and accent light on plants, water or texture. The Eclipse heater adds a dimmable light around its rim, so one fixture gives warmth and a soft glow over the table.[^s1]

| Layer | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient | Lets you see the whole space | Downlights, wall lights |
| Task | Light to work by | Over the grill or bar |
| Accent | Gives depth | Uplit planting, lit water |
| Heat-light | Warmth and glow in one | Eclipse rim lighting, dimmable |

## 3. Choose furnishings that last

Resort patios use deep seating, mixed materials and pieces that survive the weather. Choose stainless steel, teak and weatherproof fabrics, add outdoor rugs, cushions and throws, and use large planters to divide dining from lounging. A bar cart or an outdoor kitchen makes the space easy to host in.

## 4. Give the space a focal point

A fire feature, water, a piece of art or a striking heater gives people somewhere to gather. The Eclipse's arched silhouette is often the piece people notice first. At [Christopher's at Wrigley Mansion](https://www.bromic.com/case-study/christophers-at-wrigley-mansion/) in Phoenix, architect Wendell Burnette chose Eclipse freestanding heaters so the restaurant could add a patio without spoiling the historic building's design; in the live post he describes guests remarking on them.[^s4] [[VERIFY: quote wording against the case study before any direct quote is used — Megan Spies]]

## 5. Plan zones, not one big space

Dining, lounging and cooking each want their own warmth and light. Zone the heat to match, so only the part you are using is on. Freestanding heaters move with the furniture; fixed heaters suit zones that stay put. See [where to place patio heaters](item:RES-PP-01).

## 6. Make it easy to set the mood

Luxury is not hunting for switches. The Eclipse Freestanding ships with the Eclipse Dimmer Controller and remote, which step heat through 33, 66 and 100% and dim the light from your seat.[^s1][^s2] Fixed Platinum or Tungsten Electric heaters run on an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller and a wall switch. Zones, schedules and voice control come through the Bromic Smart-Heat app; Wi-Fi and app features require compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately.[^s1] Setting up: [Smart-Heat controls](item:RES-BY-01).

## Frequently asked

### What makes a patio feel luxurious?

Comfort you do not notice: warmth where you sit, soft layered light, good materials and one striking focal point.

### Can a patio heater be a design feature?

Yes. A heater that also lights the table, like the Eclipse, can be the focal point rather than something to hide.

### Do I need a wall or ceiling for a heater?

No. A freestanding heater stands on its own base and needs only power and at least 79 in. of clearance above.[^s2]
