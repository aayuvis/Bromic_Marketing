A porch is warm when heat is designed into it, not added after the ceiling is finished. On the Design Builders podcast, Bromic's Karl Tschauner and hosts Tim Matkoski and James Moylan discuss how radiant heaters are planned into screened porches and decks: sized to the space, mounted to suit the ceiling, and chosen to stay out of sight[^s1].

![Screened porch dining area with Platinum Electric heaters recessed in a cedar ceiling](img:w-hartleyhallplporchdeckdarnestownmd-0052)

## Who was on the episode?

Karl Tschauner is Vice President of Sales, North America, at Bromic Heating. The hosts run Design Builders, a firm that builds decks, screened porches and outdoor kitchens in Maryland and northern Virginia[^s1]. The episode is on the [Design Builders site](https://www.designbuildersmd.com/8152437f-9b51-476b-9a89-9ce53f636b6b/9ab4fe8b-29f4-4a7d-8e54-f41d06d8c19d)[^s2].

## Why porch builders specify radiant heat

The hosts said they first met infrared heaters in California and now include them in 95% of their porch projects[^s1] [[VERIFY: the 95% figure with Design Builders before reuse — Megan Spies]]. Tschauner explained the reason it works outdoors: infrared warms people and surfaces directly, much as sunlight does, rather than heating air that the wind carries away[^s1][^s6].

## Heat that does not compete with the design

Bromic's aim, Tschauner said, is for heaters to blend in rather than become the centrepiece[^s1]. The conversation covered the three collections:

| Collection | What it is | Where it fits on a porch |
|---|---|---|
| [Eclipse](https://www.bromic.com/eclipse-collection/) | Electric heater with a dimmable LED rim light[^s4] | Lounge and dining areas that want heat and light from one fixture |
| [Platinum](https://www.bromic.com/platinum-collection/) | Electric and gas models, plus a marine-grade electric version | Recessed flush in a finished ceiling, or coastal sites |
| [Tungsten](https://www.bromic.com/tungsten-collection/) | Higher-output electric and gas heaters with flexible mounting | Larger decks and porches, vaulted ceilings |

Each electric heater is run from Affinity Smart-Heat controls: an On/Off or Dimmer controller for Platinum and Tungsten, the Eclipse controller for Eclipse, plus a wall switch. Scheduling and zones through the Smart-Heat app need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately. Builders should price and rough in the controls with the heaters.

## Sizing and placement

The post points builders to Bromic's free [Design Your Space](https://www.bromic.com/design-your-space/) service, which reviews dimensions, ceiling height and use to recommend model, size and placement for even coverage[^s1].

## What the warranties cover

The live post said "up to 7 years on certain models". By model, from the product pages: Tungsten Electric carries 2 years on the heater and 7 years on the elements[^s3]; Eclipse Electric carries 3 years[^s4]; Platinum Electric Marine carries 5 years on land and 3 years on vessels[^s5]. ## Frequently asked

### Do infrared heaters work in a screened porch?
Yes. Radiant heat warms people and surfaces rather than air, which suits a porch that is open on several sides.

### Can heaters be recessed into a porch ceiling?
Yes. Platinum Electric and Tungsten Electric have recess kits; confirm ceiling cavity and clearances during framing.

### Who installs them?
A licensed electrician, to the manufacturer's instructions and local requirements.
