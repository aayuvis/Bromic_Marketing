On season two, episode two of Designing Outdoor Spaces, Helena Sawelin of Tillberg Design of Sweden explains how her team designs open decks on cruise ships: spaces that move, catch wind and salt, and must meet strict safety rules[^s1]. Her lesson for land-based designers is direct: design for the conditions first, then for the moment, and the guest experience follows.

![Cruise ship pool deck with loungers and planters under a white curved pergola](img:w-princess-sanctuary)

## Who is Helena Sawelin?

Helena Sawelin is a Partner and Design Director at Tillberg Design of Sweden, a studio that specialises in cruise ships and luxury yachts[^s1]. She has more than 20 years in the field and leads multidisciplinary teams from master planning down to interior and outdoor detail. The episode is hosted by Evan Troxel[^s3], co-host of Archispeak, and was released on 19 April 2025[^s2]. [[VERIFY: Helena Sawelin's current title, against Tillberg Design of Sweden — Megan Spies]]

## Designing for a space that moves

At sea, nothing stands still. Sawelin's team plans for wind, salt exposure, passenger flow and safety rules from the outset, the post reports, and chooses materials that survive harsh marine conditions[^s1]. Furniture placement has to keep working as the weather shifts or a crowd gathers. The aim she describes is comfort, safety and a memorable experience, not visual appeal alone.

## Outdoor decks as destinations

Sawelin wants decks, lounges and pool areas to be places guests choose to linger. Lighting, layout and acoustics matter as they do on land, but her team also plans around the ship's motion, the changing view, sun orientation, wind patterns and the itinerary itself[^s1]. In her account, the outdoor spaces are often the heart of the guest experience. [[VERIFY: wording of this point against the episode — Megan Spies]]

## Lessons for land-based designers

The post draws three lessons for projects on land: make the most of limited space, build atmosphere deliberately, and treat regulation as a design input rather than a late constraint[^s1].

## What it means for heating an outdoor room

| On a ship | On land | The heating decision |
|---|---|---|
| Wind across an open deck | Rooftops, waterfront terraces | Radiant heat warms people and surfaces, not air, so it copes with light wind[^s5]; add screening where wind is strong |
| Salt spray | Coastal homes and venues | Specify marine-grade units, such as Platinum Electric Marine (316 stainless, IPX5)[^s4] |
| Tight deck space | Balconies, small terraces | Recessed or ceiling-mounted heaters keep the floor clear |
| Use changes through the day | Hotel and resort terraces | Zone the heaters and set output by zone |

For salt-air settings, [Platinum Electric Marine](https://www.bromic.com/super-yacht-cruise-ships/) is the specified unit, run from an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller with a wall switch. Scheduling and zones through the Smart-Heat app need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately. Plan the controls with the heaters; they are part of the design, not an add-on.

### Do cruise-ship lessons apply to homes?
Yes, wherever a space is exposed to wind, salt or limited room. Waterfront homes and rooftop terraces face the same constraints in a milder form.
