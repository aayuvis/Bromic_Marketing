In season two, episode two of Designing Outdoor Spaces, Helena Sawelin of Tillberg Design of Sweden shares how her team designs the open decks of cruise ships, spaces that move with the sea, meet wind and salt, and must meet strict safety rules[^s1]. Her lesson for designers on land is clear: design for the conditions first, then for the moment, and a memorable guest experience will follow.

![Cruise ship pool deck with loungers and planters beneath a white curved pergola](img:w-princess-sanctuary)

## Meet Helena Sawelin

Helena Sawelin is a Partner and Design Director at Tillberg Design of Sweden, a studio that specialises in cruise ships and luxury yachts[^s1]. With more than 20 years in the field, she leads multidisciplinary teams from master planning right down to interior and outdoor detail. The episode is hosted by Evan Troxel[^s3], co-host of Archispeak, and was released on 19 April 2025[^s2]. [[VERIFY: Helena Sawelin's current title, against Tillberg Design of Sweden — Marketing]]

## Designing For A Space That Moves

At sea, nothing stands still. As the post reports, Sawelin's team plans for wind, salt exposure, passenger flow and safety rules from the very beginning, and chooses materials that endure harsh marine conditions[^s1]. Furniture placement has to keep working as the weather shifts or a crowd gathers. The aim she describes goes well beyond visual appeal, to comfort, safety and an experience guests remember.

## Decks Guests Choose To Linger On

Sawelin wants decks, lounges and pool areas to be places where guests choose to stay. Lighting, layout and acoustics matter as much as they do on land, and her team also plans around the ship's motion, the changing view, the orientation of the sun, wind patterns and the itinerary itself[^s1]. In her account, the outdoor spaces are often the heart of the guest experience. [[VERIFY: wording of this point against the episode — Marketing]]

## Lessons Designers Can Bring Ashore

The post draws a few lessons for projects on land: make the most of limited space, build atmosphere with intent, and bring regulation into the design from the outset so it shapes the space early[^s1].

## Bringing Those Lessons To Your Outdoor Room

| On a ship | On land | The heating decision |
|---|---|---|
| Wind across an open deck | Rooftops, waterfront terraces | Radiant heat warms people and surfaces, not air, so it copes with light wind[^s5]; add screening where wind is strong |
| Salt spray | Coastal homes and venues | Specify marine-grade units, such as Platinum Electric Marine (316 stainless, IPX5)[^s4] |
| Tight deck space | Balconies, small terraces | Recessed or ceiling-mounted heaters keep the floor clear |
| Use changes through the day | Hotel and resort terraces | Zone the heaters and set output by zone |

For settings by the sea, [Platinum Electric Marine](https://www.bromic.com/super-yacht-cruise-ships/) is the heater to specify, run from an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller with a wall switch. Scheduling and zones through the Smart-Heat app need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately. Plan the controls alongside the heaters from the first drawing, because they are an essential part of the design.

Yes, wherever a space is open to wind or salt, or short on room. Waterfront homes and rooftop terraces meet the same conditions in a gentler form.
