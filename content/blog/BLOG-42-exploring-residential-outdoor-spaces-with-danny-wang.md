A custom home now reads as one design from the living room to the far edge of the terrace. In season one, episode two of Designing Outdoor Spaces, designer and builder Danny Wang explains how he makes indoor and outdoor flow together, which amenities his clients now request, how he adapts to different climates, and why heating that blends in matters[^s1].

![Open-plan living opening to an alfresco area with a heater recessed in the timber ceiling](img:w-dorrington-homes-platinum-electric-1)

## Who is Danny Wang?

Danny Wang is Principal Designer of [DW Design & Build](https://www.dannywang.com/)[^s3], a turnkey design-and-build practice for high-end homes. He began as a general contractor and moved into design when the designers he worked with could not turn ideas around fast enough for his clients; he taught himself 3D design and found clients liked the bolder concepts[^s1]. He spoke with Archispeak's Evan Troxel and Cormac Phalen; the episode was released on 17 January 2024[^s2].

## One aesthetic, inside and out

Wang started with interiors and noticed clients were unhappy when the outdoor areas did not match. Now he designs both as one visual system: large doors and windows, materials, furniture and lighting that carry through, often the same tile inside and out, so the space feels larger and the transition natural[^s1].

## What clients are asking for

Wang links the shift outdoors to remote work, high property prices and a focus on well-being, which drive demand for pool houses, home offices, gyms and theatres. Pickleball courts top his clients' requests, followed by cold plunges, saunas, steam rooms, outdoor kitchens, outdoor heating and custom pool features such as sunken seating and swim-up bars[^s1].

## Adapting to climate

Working across regions with different building practices, fuels and weather, his advice is to ask local experts how things are done. Where weather is a problem he changes the plan: an indoor pool where winds are extreme, outdoor heaters in cold climates, heated driveways, retractable walls[^s1].

## Products that blend in

Wang sees better materials making integration easier, porcelain tile above all, along with improved heaters, lighting, audio and smart-home systems. He singled out Bromic heaters for their clean, slim look and integrated LED lighting, and valued products that disappear into a project[^s1].
