In a custom home today, the design flows as one from the living room to the far edge of the terrace. In season one, episode two of Designing Outdoor Spaces, designer and builder Danny Wang shares how he brings indoors and outdoors together, which amenities his clients now request, how he adapts to different climates, and why heating that blends into the space matters so much[^s1].

![Open-plan living opening onto an alfresco area, with a heater recessed in the timber ceiling](img:w-dorrington-homes-platinum-electric-1)

## Meet Danny Wang

Danny Wang is Principal Designer of [DW Design & Build](https://www.dannywang.com/)[^s3], a turnkey design-and-build practice for high-end homes. He began as a general contractor and moved into design when the designers he worked with could not turn ideas around fast enough for his clients. He taught himself 3D design and found that clients loved the bolder concepts[^s1]. He spoke with Archispeak's Evan Troxel and Cormac Phalen, and the episode was released on 17 January 2024[^s2].

## A Single Aesthetic, Inside And Out

Wang started with interiors and noticed that clients were unhappy when the outdoor areas did not match. Today he designs both as one visual system, with large doors and windows and with materials, furniture and lighting that carry through, often the same tile inside and out, so the space feels larger and the transition feels natural[^s1].

## The Amenities Clients Now Ask For

Wang links the move outdoors to remote work, high property prices and a focus on well-being, which together drive demand for pool houses, home offices, gyms and theatres. Pickleball courts top his clients' requests, followed by cold plunges, saunas, steam rooms, outdoor kitchens, outdoor heating and custom pool features such as sunken seating and swim-up bars[^s1].

## Designing For Every Climate

Working across regions with their own building practices, fuels and weather, he advises asking local experts how things are done. Where the weather presents a challenge, he adapts the plan with an indoor pool where winds are extreme, outdoor heaters in cold climates, heated driveways or retractable walls[^s1].

## Products That Disappear Into The Design

Wang sees better materials making integration easier, porcelain tile above all, along with improved heaters, lighting, audio and smart-home systems. He singled out Bromic heaters for their clean, slim look and integrated LED lighting, and he values products that disappear into a project[^s1].
