Hybrid work has settled into a weekly rhythm, so the office now competes for the days people choose to come in. Heated terraces, rooftops and courtyards help a building earn that trip: occupiers rank outdoor terraces first among the experience features that shape a lease[^s2], and electric radiant heat keeps those spaces in use on cool mornings, after work and through the shoulder months, with no gas on the roof.

![Black linear heaters on drop poles beneath a terrace canopy beside a glass facade](img:w-crown-towers-perth-bromic-tungsten-electric)

## Hybrid Has Found Its Rhythm

The debate about whether people will return has given way to a steadier question: which days, and which buildings. Across ten US cities, Kastle measured weekly office occupancy at 56.5% in November 2026, with Tuesday 10 November reaching 66.9%, its busiest peak day since the start of 2025[^s1]. Most companies now hit their targets: 72% of organizations meet their attendance goals, up from 61% a year earlier, with employers asking for 3.2 days a week and people averaging 2.9[^s2].

[[VERIFY: Kastle's weekly figure changes every week; refresh the number and the week before publishing — Digital marketing]]

That gap is where design does its work: the office people choose on a Thursday is the one with somewhere good to be over lunch and after six. For how workplace design reached this point, read [the evolution of workplace design and outdoor settings](item:BLOG-49).

## Outdoor Space Is What Occupiers Ask For

When CBRE asked occupiers which experience features shape their rent negotiations, outdoor amenities or terraces came first, at 44%, ahead of building amenity spaces and fitness facilities[^s2]. JLL reports that courtyards, roof gardens, green space and terraces remain in demand, and that offices with enhanced outdoor space regularly command rent premiums of more than 5%[^s4].

Gensler's Global Workplace Survey 2026, of 16,459 office workers in 16 countries, found that workers consistently prioritize wellness and access to nature, and point to outdoor spaces and natural light[^s3]. Research on 19,806 adults in England found that those spending at least 120 minutes a week in natural settings were more likely to report good health and high wellbeing, whether in one long visit or several short ones[^s5]. A heated terrace makes some of those minutes part of the working day.

## More Months, Longer Evenings

With radiant heat overhead, a summer lunch spot serves an early coffee in March, a team meeting in October and drinks after work in December. Radiant heat warms people and surfaces rather than the air[^s15], so it holds up in open-sided spaces where a breeze carries warm air away.

| Outdoor space | When it earns its keep | Heating approach |
|---|---|---|
| Roof terrace | Team events, client evenings, after-work gatherings | Ceiling- or wall-mounted heat over each seating group; Eclipse where there is nothing to mount on |
| Courtyard or roof garden | Breaks, one-to-ones, calls in the fresh air | Heaters on posts or pergolas, one zone per group |
| Café terrace | Breakfast, lunch, the hour after work | Heat over tables, scheduled to service hours |
| Covered loggia | Meetings and focus work through the cooler months | Recessed heaters in the soffit, dimmed to suit the day |

Every one of these heaters runs with its controls: an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer Controller (or the Eclipse Controller for Eclipse), a manual wall switch for each zone and the Bromic Smart-Heat app for schedules and timers. Wi-Fi and app features require compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately[^s12].

## All-Electric Buildings, Nothing To Burn On The Roof

In New York City, Local Law 154 restricts on-site combustion in new buildings[^s6], phased in from 2024 for buildings under seven stories and from 2027 for taller ones[^s7]. Local Law 97 sets greenhouse gas limits for most buildings over 25,000 ft², tightening from 2030[^s8]. California's 2025 Energy Code, in force from January 2026, expands heat pumps in new buildings[^s9], and Washington's commercial energy code asks that heat outside a building be radiant and controlled by an occupancy sensor or timer[^s10].

In an all-electric building there is no gas line to run to the roof, so electric radiant heat becomes the natural choice. Albany's first zero-emissions multifamily building took this route: gas fire pits and heaters were off the table, so Eclipse freestanding heaters plug into a dedicated outlet built into the roof deck, placed from Bromic heat maps[^s11]. Read the [Albany rooftop case study](https://www.bromic.com/case-study/albany-zero-emissions-multifamily-building/), and for the codes in depth, see [all-electric outdoor heating codes](item:ARC-PP-01).

## The Design Brief For Architects And Property Teams

Heat serves an office best when it is on the first set of drawings, as a system with zones and schedules.

- **Zone by seating group.** Give each table cluster, lounge or bar its own channel, so a quiet corner can stay off while the busy end stays warm. Affinity On/Off and Dimmer controllers come in 1, 2 or 5 channels at up to 6 kW per channel on 208–277 V supplies[^s13].
- **Mount at the right height.** On the current Tungsten Smart-Heat Electric range the minimums are 72 in on a wall and 96 in on a ceiling; Eclipse mounts at 96 in or more[^s14]. [[VERIFY: Tungsten Series II (Select and Coastal) minimum mounting heights — Product team]]
- **Choose the heater for the surface.** Tungsten Smart-Heat Electric Select, launched in October 2026, recesses into a low-clearance soffit or mounts on a ceiling, wall or single post, in Black, White or Graphite, warming about 64–144 ft² by model[^s12]. Eclipse Smart-Heat Electric warms about 121 ft², steps through 33, 66 and 100% output and adds dimmable rim light where the heater is part of the scene[^s12].
- **Schedule with the building.** The Affinity expansion board, built into the 5-channel controller, adds Ethernet and 0–10 V for a building management system, so the terrace follows the same hours as the lobby. In BMS mode the app is disabled[^s13], so decide early who runs the schedule. Integration compatibility varies by system[^s12].
- **Use Coastal on rooftops near the sea.** Salt air is hard on metal. Tungsten Smart-Heat Electric Coastal, also launched in October 2026, is made in 316 marine-grade stainless steel with marine-grade low-clearance recessing, ceiling, wall and single-post mounting[^s12]. Series II carries a 3-year heater warranty and 7 years on the elements[^s12].
- **Follow the code.** US electric heater circuits are installed to NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code[^s15]. Confirm circuits, mounting heights and permits with the local building authority and a licensed electrician.

## Frequently asked

### Why are offices investing in heated outdoor space?
Occupiers rank outdoor terraces first among the experience features that shape rent negotiations[^s2], and JLL reports rent premiums of more than 5% for offices with enhanced outdoor space[^s4].

### Can an all-electric office heat its roof terrace?
Yes. Electric radiant heaters need only circuits and controls, so they suit buildings with no gas supply, as Albany's zero-emissions rooftop shows[^s11].

### How are office terrace heaters controlled?
By zone and by schedule, through Affinity Smart-Heat controllers, a wall switch for each zone and the Bromic Smart-Heat app, or through the building management system with the expansion board[^s13]. Wi-Fi and app features require compatible Affinity controls, sold separately.
