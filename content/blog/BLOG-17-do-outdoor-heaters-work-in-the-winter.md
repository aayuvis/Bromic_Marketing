Yes – radiant outdoor heaters keep working through winter, because they never try to warm the cold air. They send infrared heat straight to people, furniture and floors, just as the low winter sun still warms your face on a crisp day[^s1]. Cold air and wind draw that warmth from your skin faster, so a winter patio calls for the right heater, aimed at the seating, with shelter from the wind and a cover overhead wherever you can.

![A screened porch with a vaulted timber ceiling, a fireplace and two black heaters on drop poles](img:w-dsc05737-courtney-saib)

## Why Radiant Warmth Holds Up In The Cold

A heater that warms the air stands little chance outside in winter, as the warm air rises and leaves and any breeze carries it off sooner[^s1]. A radiant heater bypasses the air altogether. Its warmth is absorbed by whatever it reaches, so you feel it even while the air around you stays cold[^s1].

What the cold does change is how quickly you lose that warmth. Moving air draws heat from exposed skin, which is why it can feel colder than the thermometer says[^s3]. The heater keeps delivering, and your space and your plan decide whether it keeps pace.

| Winter factor | What it does | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Low air temperature | Skin loses heat faster | Plan more coverage over seating; sit inside the heat pattern |
| Wind | Carries heat from skin and disperses warm air[^s3] | Screen the windward side; choose medium wave heat that carries to the table |
| Open sky | Nothing holds warm air overhead | Add a roof or soffit to trap a heat cloud[^s1] |
| Damp or coastal air | Harder on finishes and fixings | Choose materials and water ratings for the site |

## Five Ways To Enjoy Your Patio All Winter

**1. Put a roof overhead.** A soffit, roof or closed pergola holds a layer of warm air, the heat cloud, which adds to the radiant heat below. Keep at least 30% of the perimeter permanently open so it remains an outdoor space[^s1].

**2. Shelter from the wind.** Glass screens, retractable side blinds, walls and planting on the windward side all help, and wind direction is one of the first things Bromic's planning guide asks about[^s1]. For more on wind, see [do infrared heaters work outdoors](https://www.bromic.com/blog/do-infrared-heaters-work-outdoors/).

**3. Warm the seats where people gather.** Aim directional heaters at where people sit and overlap coverage where they linger longest. Coverage varies with model, mounting height, environment and installation[^s5], so let our complimentary [Design Your Space](https://www.bromic.com/design-your-space/) service draw the layout for you.

## Frequently Asked

### Can I use an outdoor heater in snow?
Heaters rated for outdoor use can run in winter weather, though you should follow the model's manual on clearances, covers and water rating, and keep snow off and away from the heater. Care tips are in [cleaning and maintenance for outdoor heaters](https://www.bromic.com/blog/keeping-the-heat-on-essential-cleaning-and-maintenance-for-outdoor-heaters/).

### Can a thermostat keep my patio at a set temperature?
No, because outdoor air cannot be held at a set point[^s1]. Control the heater's output with an Affinity controller instead.

### How many heaters do I need for winter?
More than for a mild evening on the same patio, as exposure and cold raise the warmth needed. Start from the coverage per heater and let a layout confirm it.

### Will a fire pit do the same job?
A fire pit warms the people closest to it, while overhead radiant heaters cover the whole seating area. Many patios enjoy both.
