Infrared and radiant heaters are the same thing described from two sides. "Radiant" says how the heat travels: as radiation, in straight lines, warming the people and surfaces it reaches rather than the air between. "Infrared" says what that radiation is: light just beyond red, with wavelengths from 700 nanometres to one millimetre[^s1]. Every infrared patio heater is a radiant heater. The useful question is how much of its output is radiant.

![The glowing element behind the glass face of an Eclipse heater](img:w-bromic-eclipse-portable-hotel-skansen-07)

## What do the terms actually mean?

| Term | What it describes | Outdoors |
|---|---|---|
| Radiant heat | Heat carried by electromagnetic radiation, absorbed by whatever it strikes | Reaches people directly, like sun on skin |
| Infrared | The band of radiation a heater emits, 700 nm to 1 mm[^s1] | Invisible; the glow you see is the visible tail |
| Convective heat | Heat carried by warmed air that rises and moves | Lost upward and downwind |

Bromic's Planning Guide puts it plainly: outdoor heaters move heat in one of two ways, by warming air or by infrared that heats the objects it meets[^s2]. "Infrared heater" and "radiant heater" both name the second. Retailers use the words interchangeably, and that is correct.

## Are all infrared heaters equally radiant?

No. Every heater sheds part of its energy as warm air. How much depends on wavelength, and wavelength follows how hot the element runs. Bromic's heat-wave mapping sets out the three bands[^s3]:

| | Short wave | Medium wave | Long wave |
|---|---|---|---|
| Element temperature | 3,992°F | 1,652°F | 572°F |
| Radiant / convective | 90 / 10 | 60 / 40 | 40 / 60 |
| Warm-up | Instant | About 30 s | About 5 min |
| In wind | Warms people and objects directly | Limited susceptibility | Susceptible to dispersal |
| Approx. peak wavelength (calculated) | 1.2 µm | 2.5 µm | 5.1 µm |

The first four rows are the Planning Guide's figures[^s3]. The last row is our calculation using Wien's law, which says the peak wavelength of a hot body is inversely proportional to its absolute temperature (about 2,898 µm·K divided by temperature in kelvin)[^s4]. It treats each element as an ideal emitter, so read it as a direction, not a specification.

## Why does the radiant share matter outside?

Indoors, warm air is kept by walls and a roof, so convection works. Outside, hot air rises and drifts off, and the slightest breeze takes it further. That is why forced-air heating does not work in the open and why outdoor heat cannot be run from a thermostat[^s2]. Radiation does not need the air to carry it. It lands on skin, clothing, stone and timber, and a breeze moves the air without taking back the heat already delivered[^s2].

So a 90% radiant heater sends most of its energy where people sit. A 40% radiant heater spends most of its energy warming air that leaves[^s3].

## How does Bromic engineer for it?

Radiant heating is the foundation of every Bromic heater[^s2]. The Planning Guide sets out three engineering choices behind that:

- **Direct heat.** A mix of short and medium wave gives the highest radiant output and the fastest warm-up[^s2][^s3].
- **Directional heat.** Most of the output is pushed one way, toward the seating, rather than evenly in every direction[^s2].
- **Placed for people.** Coverage is planned around where guests sit, not the floor area; it varies with model, mounting height, environment and installation[^s5].

## Frequently asked

### Is a radiant heater the same as an infrared heater?
Yes. Radiant names the way heat travels; infrared names the radiation that carries it[^s1][^s2]. An outdoor heater sold as either is warming people by radiation.

### Which type of infrared heater is best outdoors?
The most radiant one the setting allows. Short wave is about 90% radiant and warms instantly; long wave is about 40% radiant and is the most affected by wind[^s3].

### Do infrared heaters work in wind?
In light to mild wind, yes, because they warm people rather than air[^s2]. Strong, steady wind still cools skin, so screen the windward side. See [do infrared heaters work outdoors](https://www.bromic.com/blog/do-infrared-heaters-work-outdoors/).

### Can I set an outdoor infrared heater to a temperature?
Not meaningfully. Outdoor air is not a temperature you can hold[^s2]. Control output instead: on/off, dimming or schedules through Affinity Smart-Heat Controls.
