An Eclipse Freestanding heater goes together with two people and hand tools: set the base where it will stand, load its ten 22 lb weights, bolt on the arms and heater head, level the head, then plug into a 220–240 V, 20 A outlet[^s1]. A licensed electrician installs that outlet. The included Eclipse Dimmer Controller and remote then set heat and light, and the Bromic app adds schedules.

*Follow the [installation manual](https://pim.bromic.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Bromic-Eclipse-Electric-Portable-Instruction-Manual-US.pdf), which covers the Freestanding model under its earlier name, Eclipse Portable[^s4]. The outlet and circuit must be installed by a licensed electrician to the code adopted locally, [NFPA 70](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70) in most of the US[^s6]; confirm requirements with the local building authority.*

## What does the heater need on site?

| Need | Detail |
|---|---|
| Power | A 220–240 V, 20 A outlet for the heater's NEMA 6-20P plug[^s1] |
| Current | 11.4 A at 220 V, 11.8 A at 230 V, 12.1 A at 240 V[^s2] |
| Ground | Flat and level; bolt the base down in windy spots[^s1] |
| Height | The product page lists a minimum floor clearance of 79 in[^s2] [[VERIFY: what the 79 in refers to — Mike Clifford]] |
| Clearances | Side and overhead clearances are in the manual[^s2][^s4] |
| Weather | Water-resistant, not waterproof: do not leave it out in the elements[^s2] |

A 240 V outlet is not a standard patio socket. Ask a licensed electrician to add a dedicated 20 A circuit and a weather-rated NEMA 6-20R outlet near where the heater will stand, and to check GFCI requirements ([does an outdoor heater need GFCI?](item:BLD-BY-KB1)). The product page does not list a 208 V rating [[VERIFY: 208 V operation — Mike Clifford]].

## What do I need to assemble it?

- Phillips and flat-head screwdrivers
- A small step ladder and a spirit level
- Bolts for fixing the base to the ground, if you will bolt it (not supplied)
- Two strong people[^s1]

## How do I assemble it, step by step?

**Base**

1. Unscrew the base crate, lift off the base cover and set it on a soft surface.
2. Take the ten 22 lb weights out of the rear compartment and keep them close.
3. Remove the two crate screws inside the base, lift the base off the pallet and set it where the heater will stand.
4. In windy areas, bolt the base down through its five holes.
5. Put the ten weights back in the rear compartment[^s1].

**Lower arm**

6. Seat the lower arm in the base so its front holes line up with the dampeners; fix the front with two M8 washers and nuts and the rear with two M8 washers and 35 mm bolts.
7. Check the large O-ring is on the arm's connector, then slide the base cover down the arm onto the base[^s1].

**Head and upper arms**

8. Thread the cable through the top arm and connect it to the heater head; set the head angle with four dome-head screws.
9. Check the O-ring on the middle arm, feed the cable through it, and fix it to the top arm with two slotted screws.
10. With a helper, lift the head and arms onto the lower arm, feeding the cable down into the base; fix with two slotted screws.
11. Level the head with the spirit level, adjusting the dome-head screws[^s1].

**Connect and close**

12. In the connector box inside the base, join the head cable's two connectors, fit the rubber grommet and close the lid with its four screws.
13. Fix the base cover with its screw and remove all packaging[^s1].

## How do I turn it on and control it?

Plug in and switch on the outlet. The base beeps softly and the light flashes for about a second; the heater stays off until you choose a setting[^s1]. Then:

- **Eclipse Dimmer Controller and remote (included)**: set heat at 33, 66 or 100% output and dim the light, with presets you can change[^s1][^s2].
- **Bromic Smart-Heat app**: schedules, timers and zones. Wi-Fi and app features require compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately[^s3]. The product page calls the Freestanding app-enabled[^s2] [[VERIFY: whether the included controller connects to the app, or an Affinity Eclipse controller is needed — Mike Clifford]].
- **Affinity Smart-Heat Eclipse controller**: runs one Eclipse heater per channel, for owners grouping several heaters[^s5] [[VERIFY: compatibility with the plug-in Freestanding — Mike Clifford]].

The [Smart-Heat controls setup guide](item:RES-BY-01) walks through pairing, zones and schedules.

## Frequently asked

### Can I plug an Eclipse Freestanding into a normal outdoor socket?

No. It has a NEMA 6-20P plug for a 220–240 V, 20 A outlet, which a licensed electrician installs[^s1].

### Does the Eclipse Freestanding need to be bolted down?

Its weighted base holds it in normal use. In windy spots, bolt it down through the five holes in the base[^s1].

### Can it stay outside all year?

It is water-resistant, not waterproof; Bromic advises not leaving it out in the elements[^s2].

### How much area does it heat?

About 121 ft²[^s2].
