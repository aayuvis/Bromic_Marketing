Multi-family outdoor amenities are becoming everyday living space, not a pool deck used three months a year. In season one, episode three of Designing Outdoor Spaces, Sara Schneider of TCA Architects explains how her team blurs indoor and outdoor amenities, designs for year-round use while accepting weather's limits, and keeps spaces flexible enough to change as residents' needs change[^s1].

![Rooftop lounge with a row of arched freestanding Eclipse heaters under a canopy](img:w-img-3076)

## Who is Sara Schneider?

Sara Schneider is Director of Interior Design at [TCA Architects](https://tca-arch.com/)[^s3]. The live post describes more than 17 years across residential, hospitality, restaurant, winery, hotel and amenity work, covering branding, interiors and architecture[^s1]. She describes designers as problem solvers for both residents and developers. She spoke with Archispeak's Evan Troxel and Cormac Phalen; the episode was released on 12 March 2024[^s2].

## Blurring indoor and outdoor amenities

Schneider sees amenity design moving away from indoor-only rooms. Outdoor gaming and lounge areas now reach well beyond the pool deck; cabanas once treated as indoor features sit in the landscape and host barbecues and private dining; lounges, fitness areas and rooftop sky decks are designed as one connected experience[^s1].

## Designing for every season, within limits

Clients ask for spaces usable all year, and Schneider designs toward that, but she is candid that strong wind and freezing temperatures will limit use even with heaters. Her answer is to adapt: shade structures, integrated heating and careful planning to keep spaces comfortable across seasons[^s1].

## Flexibility as the brief

Resident expectations have shifted, and the same deck may serve yoga one morning and a family gathering the next. Schneider designs spaces that stay attractive and functional as preferences change[^s1].

## What it means for heat on an amenity deck

| Amenity | Heating approach |
|---|---|
| Rooftop sky deck | Radiant heat, which warms people rather than air, plus wind screening[^s5] |
| Cabana or covered lounge | Heaters recessed or ceiling-mounted in the cabana roof |
| Flexible lawn or yoga deck | Freestanding heaters that move with the layout |
| Shared dining and grill areas | Zoned heat, so one occupied table does not heat the whole deck |

Integrated heating works best when it is drawn with the structure. Recessed Platinum Electric heaters sit flush in a cabana or canopy soffit and run from an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller with a wall switch. For open decks, the Eclipse freestanding heater covers about 121 ft², rolls on integrated wheels and ships with its Eclipse Dimmer Controller and remote[^s4]. Scheduling and zones through the Smart-Heat app need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately. ## Frequently asked

### Where can I hear the episode?
On the [episode page](https://www.bromic.com/podcast/multi-residential-outdoor-spaces/), with audio links and a transcript.

### Can a rooftop amenity deck be used year-round?
More of the year, with shade, wind protection and radiant heat. Schneider is clear that extreme wind and cold still limit use.

### Should amenity heating be fixed or freestanding?
Both have a place: fixed heaters for covered structures, freestanding for open, changeable spaces.
