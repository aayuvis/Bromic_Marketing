Outdoor amenities in multi-family buildings are becoming part of everyday life, far more than a pool deck enjoyed for three months of the year. In season one, episode three of Designing Outdoor Spaces, Sara Schneider of TCA Architects shares how her team blends indoor and outdoor amenities, designs for year-round use while respecting the limits of the weather, and keeps spaces flexible enough to change as residents' needs change[^s1].

![Rooftop lounge with a row of arched freestanding Eclipse heaters beneath a canopy](img:w-img-3076)

## Meet Sara Schneider

Sara Schneider is Director of Interior Design at [TCA Architects](https://tca-arch.com/)[^s3]. The live post describes more than 17 years across residential, hospitality, restaurant, winery, hotel and amenity work, spanning branding, interiors and architecture[^s1]. She sees designers as problem solvers for residents and developers alike. She spoke with Archispeak's Evan Troxel and Cormac Phalen, and the episode was released on 12 March 2024[^s2].

## Where Indoor And Outdoor Living Meet

Schneider sees amenity design reaching well beyond indoor rooms. Outdoor gaming and lounge areas now extend far past the pool deck, cabanas once treated as indoor features sit within the landscape to host barbecues and private dinners, and lounges, fitness areas and rooftop sky decks are designed as one connected experience[^s1].

## Comfort Through The Seasons

Clients ask for spaces they can enjoy all year, and Schneider designs toward that goal while being candid that strong wind and freezing temperatures will still limit use, even with heaters. Her approach is to adapt, with shade structures, integrated heating and careful planning that keep spaces comfortable across the seasons[^s1].

## Spaces That Grow With Their Residents

Resident expectations have shifted, and the same deck may host yoga one morning and a family gathering the next. Schneider creates spaces that stay attractive and functional as preferences evolve[^s1].

## Bringing Warmth To The Amenity Deck

| Amenity | Heating approach |
|---|---|
| Rooftop sky deck | Radiant heat, which warms people rather than air, plus wind screening[^s5] |
| Cabana or covered lounge | Heaters recessed or ceiling-mounted in the cabana roof |
| Flexible lawn or yoga deck | Freestanding heaters that move with the layout |
| Shared dining and grill areas | Zoned heat, so one occupied table does not heat the whole deck |

Integrated heating works best when it is drawn alongside the structure. Recessed Platinum Electric heaters sit flush in a cabana or canopy soffit and run from an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller with a wall switch. For open decks, the Eclipse freestanding heater covers about 121 ft², rolls on integrated wheels and ships with its Eclipse Dimmer Controller and remote[^s4]. Scheduling and zones through the Smart-Heat app need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately. ## Frequently asked

### Where can I hear the episode?
You will find it on the [episode page](https://www.bromic.com/podcast/multi-residential-outdoor-spaces/), with audio links and a transcript.

### Can a rooftop amenity deck be used year-round?
With shade, wind protection and radiant heat, residents can enjoy it for far more of the year, though Schneider is clear that extreme wind and cold still limit use.

### Should amenity heating be fixed or freestanding?
Each has its place, with fixed heaters suiting covered structures and freestanding heaters suiting open spaces that change through the day.
