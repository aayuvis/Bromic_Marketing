Thoughtfully planned outdoor space sits at the heart of a sustainable building. In season one, episode five of Designing Outdoor Spaces, Thomas Ventura of Gensler explains how well-planned outdoor rooms draw on daylight and fresh air to ease the mechanical load, bring people together and endure when built from durable materials, and why all of it depends on the client embracing the goals from the very start[^s1].

![Mixed-use apartment courtyard with lounge furniture and residents enjoying the sun](img:w-gensler-project-images-3)

## Meet Thomas Ventura

Thomas Ventura is a design leader at [Gensler](https://www.gensler.com/) with more than 30 years in the industry, nearly a decade of it at Gensler, focused on mixed-use, multi-family and hospitality projects[^s1]. [[VERIFY: Thomas Ventura's current title (the episode page says Associate Design Director) against Gensler — Marketing]] The episode was released on 10 May 2024[^s2] and is hosted by Archispeak.

## It Begins With The Client

Ventura's first point is about process. When a client embraces sustainability and resilience goals from the beginning, the client becomes a partner and the project is largely on track[^s1]. He champions collaborative design within Gensler, bringing different perspectives into that early conversation.

## Outdoor Rooms That Lighten The Building's Load

The wish to connect with nature is driving demand for well-designed outdoor space, Ventura says. Used well, it brings in daylight and air that reduce artificial lighting and mechanical cooling, builds a sense of community and adds security through constant activity. He describes the building as something like a living organism that opens and closes as conditions change[^s1].

## Materials Made To Last

In high-traffic areas, durable materials reduce replacement, lower operating costs and carry the brand. Ventura connects this to Gensler's goal of making its portfolio carbon neutral by 2030[^s1][^s3], and is open about the challenge: higher-quality materials can cost more up front, so the case needs to be made to clients early.

## Comfort In Every Climate

Outdoor spaces can be as comfortable as indoor ones when they are designed around their purpose and microclimate, with views, water and connectivity. Ventura reports demand in every climate Gensler works in, and flexible outdoor rooms that serve several uses extend a building's useful space across the seasons[^s1].

## Bringing Warmth To Sustainable Outdoor Rooms

| Ventura's principle | The heating decision |
|---|---|
| Design for the microclimate | Map sun, shade and wind before placing heaters |
| Reduce the energy load | Radiant heat warms people, not air, so heat only where people sit[^s4] |
| Durable, lasting materials | Specify heaters rated for the exposure, and draw them in so they are not replaced by ad hoc units |
| Flexible, multi-use spaces | Zone heaters and schedule them to occupancy |

Outdoor heat cannot be run by thermostat[^s4], which makes thoughtful control the key to saving energy. Electric heaters such as Platinum Electric and Tungsten Electric are paired with an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller and a wall switch, and the Smart-Heat app adds zones, schedules and timers[^s5]; these features need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately. ### Where can I hear the episode?

### How do outdoor spaces make a building more sustainable?
They draw on daylight and natural air to reduce lighting and cooling, and they extend a building's usable space without enclosing and conditioning it.

### How can outdoor heating fit a low-carbon project?
Warm only the occupied zones, at the output they need and on a schedule; electric heaters can also run on a building's renewable supply.
