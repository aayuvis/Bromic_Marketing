Outdoor space is part of a sustainable building, not decoration around it. In season one, episode five of Designing Outdoor Spaces, Thomas Ventura of Gensler argues that well-planned outdoor rooms use daylight and air to ease the mechanical load, bring people together, and last when built from durable materials, and that none of it happens unless the client signs up to the goals at the start[^s1].

![Mixed-use apartment courtyard with lounge furniture and residents in the sun](img:w-gensler-project-images-3)

## Who is Thomas Ventura?

Thomas Ventura is a design leader at [Gensler](https://www.gensler.com/) with more than 30 years in the industry, nearly a decade of it at Gensler, focused on mixed-use, multi-family and hospitality projects[^s1]. [[VERIFY: Thomas Ventura's current title (the episode page says Associate Design Director) against Gensler — Megan Spies]] The episode was released on 10 May 2024[^s2] and is hosted by Archispeak.

## Sustainability starts with the client

Ventura's first point is about process. If a client buys into sustainability and resilience goals from the beginning, the client becomes a partner and the project is largely on track[^s1]. He champions collaborative design within Gensler to bring different perspectives into that early conversation.

## Outdoor rooms ease the building's load

Demand for connection with nature is driving demand for well-designed outdoor space, Ventura says. Used well, it brings in daylight and air that reduce artificial lighting and mechanical cooling, builds community, and adds security through constant activity. He describes the building as something like an organism that opens and closes as conditions change[^s1].

## Durable materials and carbon goals

In high-traffic areas, durable materials cut replacement, lower operating cost and carry the brand. Ventura links this to Gensler's goal of making its portfolio carbon neutral by 2030[^s1][^s3], and admits the hard part: higher-quality materials can cost more up front, so clients need the case made early.

## Comfort for every climate

Outdoor spaces can be as comfortable as indoor ones when they are designed for their purpose and microclimate, with views, water and connectivity. Ventura reports demand in every climate Gensler works in, and flexible outdoor rooms that serve several uses extend a building's useful space across seasons[^s1].

## What it means for heating outdoor rooms

| Ventura's principle | The heating decision |
|---|---|
| Design for the microclimate | Map sun, shade and wind before placing heaters |
| Reduce the energy load | Radiant heat warms people, not air, so heat only where people sit[^s4] |
| Durable, lasting materials | Specify heaters rated for the exposure, and draw them in so they are not replaced by ad hoc units |
| Flexible, multi-use spaces | Zone heaters and schedule them to occupancy |

Outdoor heat cannot be run by thermostat[^s4], so control is how energy is saved. Electric heaters such as Platinum Electric and Tungsten Electric run from an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller with a wall switch; the Smart-Heat app adds zones, schedules and timers[^s5] and needs compatible Affinity controls, sold separately. ### How do outdoor spaces make a building more sustainable?
By using daylight and natural air to reduce lighting and cooling, and by extending usable space without enclosing and conditioning it.

### How can outdoor heating fit a low-carbon project?
Heat only occupied zones, at the output needed, on a schedule; electric heaters can run on a building's renewable supply.
