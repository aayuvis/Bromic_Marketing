The most memorable outdoor dining spaces come from designing the site, the rules and the mood together. That idea runs through season two, episode one of Designing Outdoor Spaces, in which Frank Cavanaugh, Principal at Aria Group Architects, talks with Archispeak's Evan Troxel and Cormac Phalen about reading context, working with ordinances and health-department rules, and planning spaces that stay comfortable and inviting across the seasons[^s1].

![Covered restaurant patio with round Eclipse heaters glowing softly above the tables](img:w-stellas-wine-bar-houston-tx-3)

## Meet Frank Cavanaugh

Frank Cavanaugh is a Principal at [Aria Group](https://www.ariainc.com/)[^s3][^s4], an architecture and interiors firm known for its hospitality, entertainment and commercial work. The live post describes decades of practice and a gift for balancing aesthetics with function, so that a space works beautifully for guests and for the operator alike[^s1]. The episode was released on 4 February 2025[^s2].

## It Begins With The Setting

Before he draws a line, Cavanaugh studies the surroundings: the views, foot and vehicle traffic, ambient noise, climate, sun angles and prevailing winds[^s1]. A space in tune with its setting is far easier to make comfortable, and the same careful reading shows where shade, screening and warmth will be needed.

## Letting The Rules Shape The Design

As the post reports, zoning, building codes and health-department requirements shape the structure, the safety features and the way the space operates[^s1]. Designing them in from the first sketch spares the project a redesign later. For heating, that means confirming the electrical or gas requirements early, since US electric heaters follow NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code[^s5][^s6]. Requirements vary by jurisdiction; confirm them with your local building authority and a licensed electrician or gas fitter.

## Creating An Atmosphere Guests Remember

Cavanaugh sees ambiance as central to a space's success, with lighting, planting, water features, and materials and colours chosen for the intended mood, so guests feel at ease and want to stay[^s1].

## Spaces That Adapt And Endure

He designs spaces to adapt to the weather and to different uses, with adjustable enclosures, heating and flexible seating, and to last, with durable materials and layouts that support circulation and service[^s1].

## Where Warmth Belongs In The Plan

| Episode idea | Heating decision |
|---|---|
| Read sun and wind first | Place heaters where shade and wind leave guests cold, not where the ceiling is empty |
| Rules shape structure | Fix circuits, gas supply and mounting heights at design development |
| Ambiance matters | Choose heaters that disappear into the ceiling or double as light |
| Flexibility across seasons | Zone heaters so a half-full patio is not fully heated |

For a covered patio like the one above, Eclipse heaters hung on poles bring warmth and dimmable light from a single fixture, while recessed Platinum Electric heaters sit flush in the soffit. Each is run from Affinity Smart-Heat controls, with the Eclipse controller for Eclipse and an On/Off or Dimmer controller for Platinum, alongside a wall switch.
