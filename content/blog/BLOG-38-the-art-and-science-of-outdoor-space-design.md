Outdoor dining spaces succeed when the site, the rules and the mood are designed together. That is the thread of season two, episode one of Designing Outdoor Spaces, in which Frank Cavanaugh, Principal at Aria Group Architects, talks with Archispeak's Evan Troxel and Cormac Phalen about reading context, working with ordinances and health-department rules, and planning spaces that stay comfortable and useful across seasons[^s1].

![Covered restaurant patio with round Eclipse heaters glowing above the tables](img:w-stellas-wine-bar-houston-tx-3)

## Who is Frank Cavanaugh?

Frank Cavanaugh is a Principal at [Aria Group](https://www.ariainc.com/)[^s3][^s4], an architecture and interiors firm known for hospitality, entertainment and commercial work. The live post describes decades of practice and a habit of balancing aesthetics with function so that a space works for guests and for the operator[^s1]. The episode was released on 4 February 2025[^s2].

## Start with context

Before any drawing, Cavanaugh's approach is to study the surroundings: views, foot and vehicle traffic, ambient noise, climate, sun angles and prevailing winds[^s1]. A space that fits its setting is easier to make comfortable, and the same reading tells you where shade, screening and heat will be needed.

## Treat the rules as design input

Zoning, building codes and health-department requirements shape structure, safety features and how the space operates, the post reports[^s1]. Designing them in from the first sketch avoids redesign later. For heating, that means confirming the electrical or gas requirements early: US electric heaters follow NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code[^s5][^s6]. Requirements vary by jurisdiction; confirm them with your local building authority and a licensed electrician or gas fitter.

## Build the ambiance

Cavanaugh treats ambiance as central to success: lighting, planting, water features, and materials and colours chosen for the intended mood, so guests are comfortable and want to stay[^s1].

## Plan for flexibility and wear

Spaces should adapt to weather and to different uses, with adjustable enclosures, heating and flexible seating, and they should use durable materials and layouts that support circulation and service[^s1].

## What it means for heat in the plan

| Episode idea | Heating decision |
|---|---|
| Read sun and wind first | Place heaters where shade and wind leave guests cold, not where the ceiling is empty |
| Rules shape structure | Fix circuits, gas supply and mounting heights at design development |
| Ambiance matters | Choose heaters that disappear into the ceiling or double as light |
| Flexibility across seasons | Zone heaters so a half-full patio is not fully heated |

For a covered patio like the one above, Eclipse heaters hung on poles give heat and dimmable light from one fixture, and recessed Platinum Electric heaters sit flush in the soffit. Each is run from Affinity Smart-Heat controls: the Eclipse controller for Eclipse, an On/Off or Dimmer controller for Platinum, with a wall switch. App scheduling needs compatible Affinity controls, sold separately.
