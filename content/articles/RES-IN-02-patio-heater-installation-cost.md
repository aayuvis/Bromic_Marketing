A built-in patio heater costs the heater, its controls, the electrical or gas work and any permit. Bromic electric heaters list from $798 to $2,936 each, and their Affinity Smart-Heat® controllers from $370.[^s1][^s2][^s5] The installation is quoted by a licensed electrician or gas fitter, because the run from the panel or meter decides most of it. Price every line before the patio is finished, when the work costs least.[^s7]

## The Controls Every Heater Needs

Controls are part of every working system. Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off controllers list from $370 and Dimmer controllers from $785, each in 1, 2 and 5 channels carrying up to 6 kW a channel at 208–277 V; a manual wall switch lists from $108.[^s5] App schedules, zones and voice need compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately.[^s8]

## The Installation

| Line | What drives it | Who prices it |
|---|---|---|
| Dedicated circuit | Distance to the panel, panel capacity, open or finished ceiling | Licensed electrician |
| Mounting | Wall bracket, ceiling poles, or a recess kit and framing[^s2] | Installer |
| Gas line (built-in gas) | Run from the meter, fittings; or a propane supply | Licensed gas fitter |
| Low-voltage supply (built-in gas) | Platinum Gas needs a 110 V, 2 A feed[^s4] | Licensed electrician |
| Permit | City or county | Local building authority |

National labor estimates exist for plug-in heaters on an existing circuit: Homewyse puts that job at $437–$532 a heater, with any change to the electrical system excluded.[^s6] Bromic electric heaters run on a dedicated 208 V or 240 V circuit, so the circuit is its own line on your electrician's quote. ## Worked Example: A Covered 12 × 20 Ft Patio

- **Seating area:** 240 ft², covered, cool evenings. Factors 1.1 × 0.85 = 0.935.
- **Heaters:** Tungsten Electric 4000 W: 240 ÷ (130 × 0.935) = 1.97, so **2 heaters** (8 kW).
- **Controls:** 8 kW is more than one 6 kW channel, so a 2-channel Affinity Dimmer or On/Off controller, one heater a channel.[^s5]

## Getting More From Every Dollar

- **Wire while it is open.** Rough-in during construction costs less than opening a finished ceiling. See [pre-wiring a patio](item:BLD-IN-01).
- **Size to the seating.** Measure where people sit, not the whole slab; see [what size heater you need](item:RES-CO-KB4).
- **Choose dual-element heaters.** Tungsten 4000 W and 6000 W carry two elements, so one heater covers more and runs lighter on mild nights.[^s1]
- **Ask for a design plan.** Bromic's complimentary design service lays out heaters and controls from your photos or plans.[^s8]

## What To Take Away

- A full budget has five lines: heaters, controls, installation, permit and running cost.[^s7]
- Electric heaters list from $798 and controllers from $370 at bromic.com.[^s1][^s5]
- A dedicated 208 V or 240 V circuit is the line that varies most; get it quoted early.
- Confirm all work with your local building authority and a licensed electrician or gas fitter.

## FAQ

### How much does a patio heater cost?
Bromic built-in electric heaters list from $798 to $2,936 and freestanding heaters from $2,431, before controls and installation.[^s1][^s2][^s4]

### Do I need an electrician to install a patio heater?
For a hard-wired heater, yes. It needs a dedicated 208 V or 240 V circuit run by a licensed electrician to NFPA 70.[^s1][^s7]

### Are the controls included?
Eclipse Freestanding includes its Eclipse Dimmer Controller and remote; other heaters are paired with an Affinity controller or a manual wall switch, sold separately.[^s2][^s3][^s5]

### Is a gas patio heater cheaper to install?
It depends on how close a gas line runs. Built-in gas needs a gas fitter and, for Platinum Gas, a 110 V supply as well.[^s4]

### Do I need a permit?
Often. Ask your local building authority before the work starts.
