Yes. Bromic heaters work with Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant through compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, which connect them to the Bromic Smart-Heat app.[^s1] Once set up, you can switch heat by voice, group heaters into zones, and schedule them to match your evenings. Wi-Fi, app and voice features need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately; without them, heaters run from a manual wall switch.[^s1]

## What do I need for app and voice control?

Three layers, from the wall up.

| Layer | What it is | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| Heater | Platinum, Tungsten Electric (Coastal and Select, coming soon) or Eclipse | The heat |
| Controller | Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off, Dimmer or Eclipse controller; or a manual wall switch[^s1] | On/off; Dimmer adds output levels; Eclipse controller runs Eclipse heat and light |
| Bromic Smart-Heat app | Phone app, with compatible Affinity controls[^s1] | Adjust heat, set timers and schedules, manage zones from anywhere[^s1] |
| Voice and smart home | Amazon Alexa, Google Assistant, Control4, Crestron, Lutron[^s1] | Hands-free and whole-home control; compatibility varies and may need extra integration hardware or configuration[^s1] |

A manual wall switch is a complete, working control. It does not connect to the app or to voice assistants. [[VERIFY: which Affinity controllers are Wi-Fi enabled, and which heaters each controller supports — Mike Clifford]]

## How do I set up zones?

A zone is a group of heaters you control together — the dining table, the lounge, the outdoor kitchen. The app lets you control several areas independently.[^s1]

Plan zones around how the space is used, not where the wiring runs:

- **One zone per activity.** Dinner at the table, a drink by the fire later — heat follows you.
- **Split long spaces.** A long terrace with heaters along one side can be two zones, so a couple at one end does not heat the whole run.
- **Keep wind-exposed edges separate.** You may want them on higher when the breeze picks up.

Zones depend on the wiring and controllers installed, so decide them before the electrician starts. [[VERIFY: heaters per controller and zones per app account — Mike Clifford]] See [where to place patio heaters](item:RES-PP-01).

## How do schedules and timers work?

Schedules switch heaters on and off at set times; timers switch them off after a set period.[^s1] Use both:

- **Schedule the routine.** Heat the dining zone from 6:30 to 9:30 on weeknights, and from late afternoon at weekends.
- **Timer the extras.** A 60-minute timer for a nightcap stops heat running after everyone has gone in.
- **Change with the season.** Shift schedules as sunset moves.

Bromic positions schedules as a way to match heat to your routine and save energy.[^s1] Fewer hours is the biggest single lever on [running cost](item:RES-IN-01).

## Can I dim the heat?

On heaters run by a Dimmer or Eclipse controller, yes. Eclipse heaters step between 33%, 66% and 100% output, and their rim lighting dims too.[^s1] An On/Off controller or wall switch gives full heat or none. [[VERIFY: Dimmer controller output levels for Platinum and Tungsten Electric — Mike Clifford]]

Dimming matters because outdoor heat has no thermostat: there is no room temperature to hold.[^s2] You set the level to the evening — low on a mild night, full when the wind picks up.

## How do I connect Alexa or Google Assistant?

The Lookbook confirms Alexa and Google Assistant work with Bromic heaters through compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls.[^s1] The general order is:

1. Install compatible Affinity controls and connect them to Wi-Fi. [[VERIFY: connection method and network requirements — Mike Clifford]]
2. Set up heaters and zones in the Bromic Smart-Heat app.
3. Link the app to Alexa or Google Assistant from the assistant's app. [[VERIFY: skill/action name and linking steps — Mike Clifford]]
4. Name zones clearly — "dining heaters", "lounge heaters" — so voice commands are easy.

## What about Control4, Crestron or Lutron?

Affinity controls are listed as compatible with Control4, Crestron and Lutron. Compatibility varies by system and may need additional integration hardware or configuration, so involve your integrator early.[^s1]

## Your first-week setup checklist

The first week decides whether controls get used or forgotten. Work through this list once, with the installer still on call.

- **Day one:** test every heater from its controller or wall switch, and note which switch runs which heater.
- **Name the zones** in the app after the places you use — table, lounge, kitchen — not the circuit numbers.
- **Set one weekday schedule and one weekend schedule.** Adjust after a few evenings outside.
- **Add a timer** for late nights, so heat stops when you go in.
- **Link your voice assistant** and try a few commands from the patio, where you will actually use them.
- **Show everyone at home** how to switch heat on and off by hand, without the app.
- **Keep the installation manual** and the controller model numbers with your house documents.

## Key takeaways

- Every heater needs a control: an Affinity On/Off, Dimmer or Eclipse controller, or a manual wall switch.
- App, schedules, zones and voice need compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately.[^s1]
- Plan zones around activities, before wiring.
- Schedules and timers cut wasted hours; dimming matches heat to the night.
- Alexa and Google Assistant work through the app; Control4, Crestron and Lutron may need extra hardware.[^s1]
