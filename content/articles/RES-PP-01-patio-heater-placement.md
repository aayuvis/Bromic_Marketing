Bromic's guidance is to mount an outdoor electric heater at least 72" (6 ft) above the floor on a wall, and at least 96" (8 ft) on a ceiling.[^s1] These are minimums. The right height for your heater depends on its output, and the installation manual for your model sets the exact figure, along with the clearance it needs from the ceiling and from anything that can burn.[^s1] Within those limits, place each heater so its warmth falls on the people seated around the table, rather than on the floor in front of them.

## Finding The Right Height

| Mount | Bromic minimum (floor to heater) | What changes it |
|---|---|---|
| Wall, electric | 72" / 6 ft[^s1] | Heater output; the model's installation manual |
| Ceiling, electric | 96" / 8 ft[^s1] | Heater output; clearance from the ceiling varies with output[^s1] |
| Recessed, electric (Platinum, Tungsten Electric) | Per installation manual [[VERIFY: recessed mounting heights by model — Product team]] | Recess kit and ceiling cavity |
| Eclipse pendant | Per installation manual [[VERIFY: Eclipse mounting height and pole selection — Product team]] | Pole: single 8", 24" or 48", double or curved[^s2] |
| Tungsten Freestanding Gas | Freestanding; no mounting height | Clearance to combustibles and overhead, per manual [[VERIFY — Product team]] |

A higher heater is not always a warmer one. Coverage depends on mounting height as well as on the model, the environment and the installation.[^s2] Mount it too high and less of the warmth reaches your seating; mount it too low and it falls below its minimum. Under a tall ceiling, suspension poles bring Platinum and Tungsten Electric heaters down to an effective height.[^s1]

Electric installation follows ANSI/NFPA 70 (the National Electrical Code), and gas installation follows ANSI Z223.1/NFPA 54.[^s1] Requirements vary by city and county, so confirm yours with your local building authority and work with a licensed electrician or gas fitter.

## Aiming The Warmth At Your Guests

Every Bromic heater is directional, sending most of its heat one way, so it should always face the seating.[^s1] A few principles keep everyone comfortable:

- **Aim at shoulders and laps**, rather than the table top or the middle of the floor.
- **Keep the line of sight clear.** Infrared travels in straight lines, so an umbrella, beam or planter between the heater and a guest casts a cold shadow.
- **Warm long tables and wide lounges from both sides**, so no one is left with a cold back.

Wall heaters angle their warmth down across the space, while ceiling and recessed heaters bloom it down onto the seating. Choose the mount that reaches your guests first, and then the look that suits your outdoor space.

## Spacing Heaters So Every Seat Is Warm

Space your heaters so their coverage areas meet and slightly overlap over the seating, leaving no cold seams between chairs. Each heater's approximate coverage is the place to start:

| Heater | Approx. coverage per heater[^s2] |
|---|---|
| Platinum Smart-Heat Electric (2300/3400/4500 W) | 65–143 ft² |
| Tungsten Smart-Heat Electric, Coastal and Select | 64–144 ft² |
| Eclipse Smart-Heat Electric / Freestanding Electric | 121 ft² |
| Tungsten Smart-Heat Freestanding Gas | 215 ft² |

Take a 24' × 10' dining terrace of 240 ft² as a worked example. Count heaters as area ÷ (coverage × cover factor × climate factor), rounded up; for a partly covered terrace on cool evenings the two factors come to 1.0 × 0.85 = 0.85 (an open site is 0.85 for cover, a solid roof 1.1; mild evenings 1.0, cold 0.7). [[VERIFY: cover and climate factors — Product team]] With Platinum 4500W, 240 ÷ (143 × 0.85) = 2.0, so you would plan two; with Platinum 3400W, 240 ÷ (108 × 0.85) = 2.6, so three. Run them along the long side, each centred over its own section of the table with the edges overlapping, and give each its Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller and a wall switch. A freestanding heater serves at most four seats, because guests block its radiant heat. At 20 ft² a seat, 240 ft² seats 12, which calls for three Tungsten Freestanding Gas heaters. ## Understanding Clearance To Combustibles

Clearance to combustibles is the minimum distance between a heater and anything that can burn, from timber ceilings, soffits and pergola beams to awnings, curtains and cushions. Each model's installation manual gives its clearances above, beside and in front of the heater, and the clearance to the ceiling varies with output.[^s1] [[VERIFY: clearance-to-combustibles table by model — Product team]]

There are several elegant ways to design within it:

- **With gas heaters**, dual-layer stainless heat deflectors reduce the clearance needed above the heater, and ceiling poles mount two units back to back under flat or pitched ceilings.[^s1]
- **With electric heaters**, recess kits flush-mount Platinum and Tungsten Electric into a roof cavity, built to the recess clearances in the manual.[^s1] The Tungsten Select and Coastal collections add low-clearance recessing.[^s2]
- **Around soft furnishings**, keep fabric, umbrellas and cushions out of the heat path. As general home-heating guidance, NFPA advises keeping anything that can burn at least three feet from heating equipment.[^s3] [[VERIFY: NFPA tip wording; heater-specific distances come from the manual — Marketing]]

## How A Covered Patio Helps

A partial cover works in your favor, holding a "heat cloud" over the seating that adds a gentle convective boost.[^s1] The space still has to count as outdoor, with at least 30% of its perimeter permanently open, and the heater must clear the ceiling by the distance its manual sets.[^s1] Our answer to [can I put a patio heater under a covered patio](item:RES-PP-KB1) explains more.

## Before The First Hole Is Drilled

- Mark where people will sit, because that is where the warmth needs to land.
- Confirm the mounting surface, whether a wall, ceiling, recess cavity or pole.
- Check the minimum heights of 72" on a wall and 96" on a ceiling (Bromic guidance), then the model's manual.[^s1]
- Check the clearances to ceilings, beams, awnings and fabrics in the manual.
- Space the heaters so their coverage overlaps over the seating.
- Plan the controls alongside the wiring, with an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off, Dimmer or Eclipse controller, or a wall switch; app zones and schedules need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately.[^s2] Our guide to [setting up Smart-Heat controls](item:RES-BY-01) walks you through it.
- Confirm requirements with your local building authority and a licensed electrician or gas fitter.

## FAQ

### What is the minimum height for a wall-mounted patio heater?
Bromic guidance is at least 72" (6 ft) above the floor, and the model's installation manual sets the exact figure for its output.[^s1]

### How high should a ceiling-mounted patio heater be?
At least 96" (8 ft) above the floor, with a clearance from the ceiling that varies with output.[^s1]

### Can I mount a patio heater under a wooden pergola?
Often you can, provided the heater meets its clearance to combustibles. Recess kits, suspension poles and, for gas, heat deflectors all help, and the manual always governs.[^s1]

### How many heaters for a 20 × 12 patio?
For 240 ft², partly covered and used on cool evenings, you would plan two Platinum 4500W heaters (240 ÷ (143 × 0.85) = 2.0) or three 3400W (240 ÷ (108 × 0.85) = 2.6), each with Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controls and a wall switch.[^s2] Freestanding gas is sized by seats rather than area. At 20 ft² a seat the patio holds 12 seats, and at no more than four seats a heater, that means three Tungsten Freestanding Gas heaters. An open, windy or colder site may need one more overhead heater.

### Should heaters face each other?
On wide spaces, warming from two sides keeps everyone comfortable. Let the coverage overlap across the seating, rather than doubling it up in one spot.
