Bromic's guidance is to mount an outdoor electric heater at least 72" (6 ft) above the floor on a wall, and at least 96" (8 ft) on a ceiling.[^s1] Those are minimums, and the right height for your heater depends on its output — the installation manual for your model sets the exact figure, along with the clearance it needs from the ceiling and from anything that can burn.[^s1] Within those limits, place heaters so their warmth lands on the people sitting down, not on the floor in front of them.

## How high should I mount an outdoor heater?

| Mount | Bromic minimum (floor to heater) | What changes it |
|---|---|---|
| Wall, electric | 72" / 6 ft[^s1] | Heater output; the model's installation manual |
| Ceiling, electric | 96" / 8 ft[^s1] | Heater output; clearance from the ceiling varies with output[^s1] |
| Recessed, electric (Platinum, Tungsten Electric) | Per installation manual [[VERIFY: recessed mounting heights by model — Mike Clifford]] | Recess kit and ceiling cavity |
| Eclipse pendant | Per installation manual [[VERIFY: Eclipse mounting height and pole selection — Mike Clifford]] | Pole: single 8", 24" or 48", double or curved[^s2] |
| Tungsten Freestanding Gas | Freestanding; no mounting height | Clearance to combustibles and overhead, per manual [[VERIFY — Mike Clifford]] |

Higher is not automatically better. Coverage depends on mounting height as well as model, environment and installation.[^s2] Too high and less heat reaches the seating; too low and the heater breaks its minimum. On a tall ceiling, suspension poles bring Platinum and Tungsten Electric heaters down to an effective height.[^s1]

Electric installation follows ANSI/NFPA 70 (the National Electrical Code); gas follows ANSI Z223.1/NFPA 54.[^s1] Requirements vary by city and county — confirm with your local building authority and use a licensed electrician or gas fitter.

## Where should heaters point?

At the seating. Bromic heaters are directional: most of their heat goes one way.[^s1] Three rules:

- **Aim at shoulders and laps**, not the table top or the middle of the floor.
- **Keep a clear line of sight.** Infrared travels in straight lines; an umbrella, beam or planter between heater and guest casts a cold shadow.
- **Heat from two sides** on long tables or wide lounges, so no one has a cold back.

Wall heaters angle down across the space; ceiling and recessed heaters bloom down onto it. Pick the mount that reaches the seating, then the look.

## How far apart should patio heaters be?

Space them so their coverage areas meet and slightly overlap over the seating — no cold seams between chairs. Start from each heater's approximate coverage:

| Heater | Approx. coverage per heater[^s2] |
|---|---|
| Platinum Smart-Heat Electric (2300/3400/4500 W) | 65–143 ft² |
| Tungsten Smart-Heat Electric, Coastal and Select (coming soon) | 64–144 ft² |
| Eclipse Smart-Heat Electric / Freestanding Electric | 121 ft² |
| Tungsten Smart-Heat Freestanding Gas | 215 ft² |

A worked example: a 24' × 10' dining terrace is 240 ft². With heaters near the middle of the Platinum range, that is two to three units along the long side, each centred over a section of the table, edges overlapping. ## What is clearance to combustibles?

The minimum distance between a heater and anything that can burn — timber ceilings, soffits, pergola beams, awnings, curtains, cushions. Each model's installation manual gives its clearances above, beside and in front of the heater; ceiling clearance varies with output.[^s1] [[VERIFY: clearance-to-combustibles table by model — Mike Clifford]]

Ways to work within it:

- **Gas heaters:** dual-layer stainless heat deflectors reduce the clearance needed above the heater; ceiling poles mount two units back to back under flat or pitched ceilings.[^s1]
- **Electric heaters:** recess kits flush-mount Platinum and Tungsten Electric into a roof cavity, built to the recess clearances in the manual.[^s1] The coming-soon Tungsten collections add low-clearance recessing.[^s2]
- **Soft furnishings:** keep fabric, umbrellas and cushions out of the heat path. As general home-heating guidance, NFPA advises keeping anything that can burn at least three feet from heating equipment.[^s3] [[VERIFY: NFPA tip wording; heater-specific distances come from the manual — Megan Spies]]

## Does a covered patio change placement?

It helps. A partial cover holds a "heat cloud" over the seating that adds a convective boost.[^s1] The space still has to count as outdoor — at least 30% of its perimeter permanently open — and the heater must clear the ceiling by the distance its manual sets.[^s1] See [can I put a patio heater under a covered patio](item:RES-PP-KB1).

## Planning checklist

- Mark where people will sit; that is the heat target.
- Confirm the mounting surface: wall, ceiling, recess cavity or pole.
- Check minimum heights: 72" wall, 96" ceiling (Bromic guidance), then the model's manual.[^s1]
- Check clearances to ceilings, beams, awnings and fabrics in the manual.
- Space heaters so coverage overlaps over the seating.
- Plan controls with the wiring: an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off, Dimmer or Eclipse controller, or wall switch; app zones and schedules need compatible Affinity controls, sold separately.[^s2] See [setting up Smart-Heat controls](item:RES-BY-01).
- Confirm requirements with your local building authority and a licensed electrician or gas fitter.

## FAQ

### What is the minimum height for a wall-mounted patio heater?
Bromic guidance is at least 72" (6 ft) above the floor; the model's installation manual sets the exact figure for its output.[^s1]

### How high should a ceiling-mounted patio heater be?
At least 96" (8 ft) above the floor, with clearance from the ceiling that varies with output.[^s1]

### Can I mount a patio heater under a wooden pergola?
Often, if the heater meets its clearance to combustibles. Recess kits, suspension poles and (for gas) heat deflectors help; the manual governs.[^s1]

### How many heaters for a 20 × 12 patio?
240 ft² usually takes two to three Platinum heaters, or one or two freestanding gas heaters, depending on climate, wind and mounting height.[^s2]

### Should heaters face each other?
On wide spaces, heating from two sides keeps everyone warm. Overlap the coverage over the seating rather than doubling it in one spot.
