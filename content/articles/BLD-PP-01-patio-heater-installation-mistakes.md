A patio heater that is not warm enough is usually mounted too high or too far from the seating, covering more area than it can in that wind, or running on the wrong voltage. A heater that keeps tripping is usually on an undersized or shared circuit. All of these are decided before the heater is hung, which is why they come back as callbacks. Here are the five mistakes, and how to design them out.

*Mounting heights, clearances and wiring must follow the product installation manual, the local building authority and a licensed electrician.*

## What are the five mistakes that cause callbacks?

| Mistake | What the homeowner says | Prevent it at |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Mounted too high, or too low | "I can't feel it" or "it's too hot on my head" | Plan: fix heights from the manual |
| 2. Too few heaters for the exposure | "Warm at the table, cold at the edge" | Design: size for wind and climate |
| 3. Clearances not kept | Scorched trim, a failed inspection, a moved heater | Framing: mark clearances before boarding |
| 4. Undersized or wrong-voltage circuit | "It trips" or "it's weaker than the showroom" | Rough-in: 125% sizing, matched voltage |
| 5. Controls not planned | "There's no switch" or "the app won't connect" | Plan: controller on the electrical drawing |

## 1. Why does mounting height matter so much?

Radiant heat falls off with distance. Mount a heater higher than its rating allows and the people underneath feel little. Mount it too low and heads get hot, and you may breach the code minimum.

Bromic's guidance is at least 72 in. above the floor for wall mounts and at least 96 in. for ceiling mounts, with the right height varying by output[^s2]. Approximate coverage also changes with mounting height[^s1]. Take the height for each model from the installation manual [[VERIFY: recommended mounting height range per model and wattage — Mike Clifford]], and on high ceilings use suspension poles to bring the heater down to an effective height[^s2].

## 2. How many heaters does an exposed patio need?

More than a sheltered one. Platinum Smart-Heat Electric covers approx. 65–143 ft² per heater and Eclipse approx. 121 ft², but those are approximate and depend on environment and installation[^s1]. Three things push the count up:

- **Openness.** An area counts as outdoor when at least 30% of its perimeter is open. A partial roof traps a "heat cloud" that helps; a fully open deck gets none of it[^s2].
- **Wind and water.** Radiant heat works in light wind, but a breezy, waterside site needs more heat on the people[^s2].
- **Climate.** The same patio can need more heaters in a cold state than in a mild one[^s2].

Size for the coldest evening the buyer will use the space, and cover where people sit, not just the slab. Bromic's complimentary design service will draw coverage for a plan.

## 3. Which clearances get missed?

The ones nobody marked before the boards went up: distance to the ceiling above a wall heater, to beams and fans beside a ceiling heater, and to combustible trim. The clearance from the ceiling varies with output[^s2]. Pull the clearance table from the installation manual for the exact model [[VERIFY: clearance table per model — Mike Clifford]] and mark it on the framing. A fan, light or speaker placed inside a heater's clearance is a redesign, not a fix.

## 4. Why does a heater trip, or feel weak?

Two electrical faults cause most of these calls.

**Undersized circuits.** An outdoor heater can run for hours, so treat it as a continuous load: current expected for three hours or more[^s3]. Size the branch circuit at no less than 125% of the heater's marked current[^s3]. A 4500 W heater at 240 V draws 18.75 A, or 23.4 A with the 125% rule: it does not belong on a 20 A circuit. Heaters added to a shared circuit trip it.

**Wrong voltage.** A heater built for 240 V on a 208 V supply delivers about 75% of its rated output, because output falls with the square of the voltage. That is a heater that "works" but never feels right. Confirm the site voltage and order the matching model [[VERIFY: voltage options per model — Mike Clifford]].

See the [rough-in checklist](item:BLD-IN-01) and [heaters per circuit](item:BLD-IN-KB2) for the maths.

## 5. What goes wrong with controls?

Outdoor heat cannot be thermostatically controlled[^s2], so people control it by hand. If the controller is not on the plan, the heater ends up on a bare breaker or a switch in the wrong place.

Every heater needs its control: an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller (Eclipse controller for Eclipse) and a manual wall switch[^s1]. Put the controller by the patio door. If the buyer expects app control, schedules or voice, they need compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately; smart-home integration may need extra hardware or configuration[^s1]. Promise only what is in the package.

## How do builders keep supply from causing the sixth problem?

Order heaters, recess kits and controls together, against the rough-in date, not the trim date. A recess kit that arrives after the ceiling is boarded is mistake three by another route. Lead times come from the builder program [[VERIFY: standard lead times by product and region — Arvin Avaness / Peter Abraham]].

## Planning checklist

- Heights and clearances for each model taken from the installation manual and marked on framing.
- Heater count sized for openness, wind and the coldest evening of use; design service drawing on file.
- Site voltage confirmed; voltage-matched heaters ordered.
- One dedicated circuit per heater, sized at 125%, within the nameplate maximum.
- Controller and wall switch on the electrical plan by the patio door.
- App and smart-home features sold only with compatible Affinity controls.
- Heaters, recess kits and controls ordered against the rough-in date.

## FAQ

### Why is my patio heater not warm enough?
Most often it is mounted too high, too far from the seating, asked to cover more area than it can in that exposure, or running on 208 V when it was built for 240 V.

### How high should an electric patio heater be mounted?
At least 72 in. for a wall mount and 96 in. for a ceiling mount, per Bromic's guidance, with the exact height set by the model's output and its installation manual.

### Can wind make a radiant heater feel weak?
Light wind has less effect on radiant heat than on forced air, but an open, windy site needs more heaters to keep people warm.

### Why does my patio heater keep tripping the breaker?
Usually the circuit is undersized for a continuous load or shared with other heaters. Size each circuit at 125% of the heater's current.

### Do I need a controller for every heater?
Every heater needs a control: an Affinity Smart-Heat controller and a wall switch. How many heaters one controller can run depends on the controller and its load rating [[VERIFY: heaters and maximum load per Affinity controller — Mike Clifford]].
