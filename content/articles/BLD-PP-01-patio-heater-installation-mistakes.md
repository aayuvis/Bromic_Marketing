When a homeowner says their patio heater is not warm enough, the cause is usually set long before the first evening outdoors: a heater mounted too high or too far from the seating, asked to cover more area than it can in that wind, or running on the wrong voltage. A heater that keeps tripping is most often on an undersized or shared circuit. Each of these is decided at plan and rough-in, which is exactly where you can design them out.

*Mounting heights, clearances and wiring must follow the product installation manual, the local building authority and a licensed electrician.*

## The Five Mistakes Behind Most Callbacks

| Mistake | What the homeowner says | Prevent it at |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Mounted too high, or too low | "I can't feel it" or "it's too hot on my head" | Plan: fix heights from the manual |
| 2. Too few heaters for the exposure | "Warm at the table, cold at the edge" | Design: size for wind and climate |
| 3. Clearances not kept | Scorched trim, a failed inspection, a moved heater | Framing: mark clearances before boarding |
| 4. Undersized or wrong-voltage circuit | "It trips" or "it's weaker than the showroom" | Rough-in: 125% sizing, matched voltage |
| 5. Controls not planned | "There's no switch" or "the app won't connect" | Plan: controller on the electrical drawing |

## 1. Setting The Height That Puts Warmth On People

Radiant heat softens with distance, so the height of a heater shapes how the whole space feels. Mount it higher than its rating allows and the people beneath it feel very little; mount it too low and heads get uncomfortably warm, and the installation may fall below the code minimum.

Bromic's guidance is at least 72 in. above the floor for wall mounts and at least 96 in. for ceiling mounts, with the right height varying by output[^s2]. Approximate coverage also changes with mounting height[^s1]. Take the height for each model from the installation manual [[VERIFY: recommended mounting height range per model and wattage — Product team]], and under high ceilings use suspension poles to bring the heater down to an effective height[^s2].

## 2. Sizing For The Coldest Evening, Including The Breeze

An exposed patio needs more heaters than a sheltered one. Platinum Smart-Heat® Electric covers approx. 65–143 ft² per heater and Eclipse Smart-Heat® approx. 121 ft², and both figures are approximate, depending on environment and installation[^s1]. Several things push the count up:

- **How open the space is.** An area counts as outdoor when at least 30% of its perimeter is open. A partial roof holds a "heat cloud" that helps, while a fully open deck gets none of that benefit[^s2].
- **Wind and water.** Radiant heat works well in light wind, though a breezy, waterside site needs more heat directed onto the people[^s2].
- **Climate.** The same patio can need more heaters in a cold state than in a mild one[^s2].

Size for the coldest evening the homeowner plans to spend outside, and cover the places where people actually sit as well as the slab itself. Bromic's complimentary design service will draw the coverage for your plan.

## 3. Marking Clearances Before The Boards Go Up

The clearances that get missed are usually the ones nobody marked before the framing was closed in: the distance to the ceiling above a wall heater, to beams and fans beside a ceiling heater, and to combustible trim. The clearance from the ceiling varies with output[^s2]. Pull the clearance table from the installation manual for the exact model [[VERIFY: clearance table per model — Product team]] and mark it on the framing. A fan, light or speaker placed inside a heater's clearance means a redesign once the ceiling is finished, so it pays to settle the layout early.

## 4. Building A Circuit That Carries The Load

Two electrical faults sit behind most of these calls, and both are simple to avoid at rough-in.

**Undersized circuits.** An outdoor heater can run for hours, so treat it as a continuous load, meaning current expected for three hours or more[^s3]. Size the branch circuit at no less than 125% of the heater's marked current[^s3]. A 4500 W heater at 240 V draws 18.75 A, or 23.4 A with the 125% rule, so it belongs on a circuit larger than 20 A. Adding heaters to a shared circuit is what makes it trip.

**Matching the voltage.** A heater built for 240 V on a 208 V supply delivers about 75% of its rated output, because output falls with the square of the voltage. The heater still runs, yet it never quite feels the way the homeowner expected. Confirm the site voltage and order the matching model [[VERIFY: voltage options per model — Product team]].

## 5. Placing Controls Where People Reach For Them

Outdoor heat cannot be thermostatically controlled[^s2], so people adjust it by hand through the evening. When the controller is left off the plan, the heater tends to end up on a bare breaker or a switch in an awkward place.

Every heater is paired with its control: an Affinity Smart-Heat® On/Off or Dimmer controller (the Eclipse controller for Eclipse) and a manual wall switch[^s1]. Place the controller by the patio door, where it falls naturally to hand. If the homeowner expects app control, schedules or voice, they need compatible Affinity Smart-Heat® Controls, sold separately, and smart-home integration may need extra hardware or configuration[^s1]. Promise only what is in the package, and the first evening will match the conversation you had at handover.

## FAQ

### Why is my patio heater not warm enough?
Most often it is mounted too high or too far from the seating, asked to cover more area than it can in that exposure, or running on 208 V when it was built for 240 V.

### How high should an electric patio heater be mounted?
Bromic's guidance is at least 72 in. for a wall mount and 96 in. for a ceiling mount, with the exact height set by the model's output and its installation manual.

### Can wind make a radiant heater feel weak?
Light wind has less effect on radiant heat than on forced air, though an open, windy site needs more heaters to keep everyone comfortable.

### Why does my patio heater keep tripping the breaker?
Usually the circuit is undersized for a continuous load or shared with other heaters. Size each circuit at 125% of the heater's current.

### Do I need a controller for every heater?
Every heater needs a control: an Affinity Smart-Heat controller and a wall switch. How many heaters one controller can run depends on the controller and its load rating [[VERIFY: heaters and maximum load per Affinity controller — Product team]].
