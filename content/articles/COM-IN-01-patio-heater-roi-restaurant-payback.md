Patio heater ROI is the margin earned from seats you could not fill before, less the cost of warming them, set against the installed cost. Calculate added revenue as heated seats × turns per night × average check × extra nights × share of seats filled; apply your contribution margin, subtract running cost, and divide the installed cost by the monthly result. That gives you payback in months, the figure an owner, investor or landlord will want to see before they sign.

## The Numbers Behind A Warmer Patio

The case rests on five revenue inputs, two cost inputs and one margin, and every one of them comes from your own business.

| Input | What it means | Where to get it |
|---|---|---|
| Heated seats | Seats inside heated coverage | Your floor plan |
| Turns per night | Seatings per seat per evening | POS, by daypart |
| Average check | Revenue per guest | POS |
| Extra nights | Nights a year the patio opens that it would not unheated | Last year's closures and weather |
| Share of seats filled | Share of heated seats you actually fill on those nights | Your judgement; start low |
| Contribution margin | Share of revenue left after food, drink and variable labour | Your P&L |
| Running cost | Energy for the heated hours | Rated input × hours × your tariff |
| Installed cost | Heaters, controls, wiring or gas, permits | A full quote |

Share of seats filled is where honesty matters most. A heated patio on a cold Tuesday will rarely be full, and a case priced at 100% is the quickest way for a capital request to lose credibility.

## Working Out Your Payback

1. **Added revenue** = heated seats × turns × average check × extra nights × share of seats filled
2. **Added margin** = added revenue × contribution margin
3. **Net annual gain** = added margin − annual running cost
4. **Payback (months)** = installed cost ÷ (net annual gain ÷ 12)

Extra nights is the simplest case. If the heaters instead lengthen evenings you already open, keeping the patio full after 8 pm, use extra hours and covers per hour; the logic stays the same.

## One Patio, Worked Through

This example is illustrative, with every input chosen to show the method, and it should be read as neither a benchmark nor a promise. [[VERIFY: benchmarks — Hospitality sales]]

**Assumptions:** 40 heated seats; 1.5 turns a night; $45 average check; 60 extra nights a year; 50% of seats filled; 30% contribution margin; seven Platinum Smart-Heat® Electric 4500W heaters with Affinity Smart-Heat® On/Off or Dimmer controls and a wall switch, running 5 hours a night on those nights; electricity at 14.07¢/kWh, the US commercial average for January–May 2026[^s2]; installed cost $35,000 (7 × $5,000, a placeholder for a full quote including controls, wiring and permits, not a Bromic price). [[VERIFY: installed cost per heater — Hospitality sales]]

Seven heaters is the sizing for 40 seats: 40 × 20 ft² a seat = 800 ft²[^s5], ÷ (143 ft² coverage[^s3] × 0.85 for a partly covered patio on cool evenings) = 6.6, rounded up to 7. See [how many heaters a 40-seat patio needs](item:COM-IN-KB1).

- Added revenue: 40 × 1.5 × $45 × 60 × 0.5 = **$81,000 a year**
- Added margin: $81,000 × 30% = **$24,300**
- Running cost: 7 × 4.5 kW × 5 h × 60 nights = 9,450 kWh × $0.1407 = **$1,330**
- Net annual gain: $24,300 − $1,330 = **$22,970**, or about $1,914 a month
- Payback: $35,000 ÷ $1,914 = **about 18.3 months**

In this example the running cost is small beside the margin. The inputs that move payback most are the share of seats filled and the number of extra nights.

## How Payback Shifts With Your Assumptions

Payback is very sensitive to how full the patio is. The table keeps the same example and changes only the share of seats filled.

| Seats filled (assumption) | Added revenue | Net annual gain | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25% | $40,500 | $10,820 | about 38.8 months |
| 50% | $81,000 | $22,970 | about 18.3 months |
| 75% | $121,500 | $35,120 | about 12.0 months |

A one-year payback needs about 75% of the heated seats filled.

Share this table with whoever signs. A case that still pays back at the low end is the one that earns approval.

## Details That Are Easy To Overlook

- **The full installed cost.** Heaters are one line among several. Controls, circuits or gas line, mounting, permits and any storage for propane belong in it as well, and Bromic recommends quoting all-in.[^s4]
- **Controls.** Each heater works as a complete system with its controls: an Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controller and a wall switch for Platinum, and the Eclipse controller for Eclipse. Wi-Fi and app features – zones, schedules, timers – require compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately.[^s3]
- **The hours you actually heat.** Zoning by section and scheduling to the book keep running cost tied to covers.
- **Fuel handling.** Propane adds cylinder labour and storage, set out in [the five-year cost comparison](item:COM-CO-01).
- **Permits and lead time.** Heaters earn only once they are signed off for the season, so plan with [the permit checklist](item:COM-PP-01).

## Presenting The Case To An Owner Or Landlord

Keep it to one page. Lead with the payback and the low-case payback, set the inputs and their sources beneath, and attach the quote. Patios carry real weight in many restaurants' sales – in a 2021 National Restaurant Association survey, 35% of operators with outdoor seating said it accounted for more than 40% of average daily sales[^s1] – so present the heaters as added capacity for the business. Landlords will also want the installation method, fuel and code sign-off, which belong in the same pack.

> Code and permit requirements vary by city and change over time. Confirm with your local building and fire authority, and use a licensed electrician or gas fitter.

## What To Take Away

- ROI is the margin from new covers, less running cost, set against installed cost.
- Share of seats filled and extra nights drive the answer, so begin with conservative figures.
- Running cost is usually small beside margin, and it still belongs in the sum.
- Quote all-in, including controls and permits.
- Show the low case, because it is what wins the budget.

## FAQ

### How do I calculate ROI on restaurant patio heaters?

Added revenue = heated seats × turns × average check × extra nights × share of seats filled. Multiply by contribution margin, subtract annual running cost, and divide installed cost by the monthly result for payback in months.

### What share of seats filled should I assume?

Your own, drawn from past shoulder-season nights. If you have none yet, run the case at a low, mid and high rate and present all three.

### Should I use revenue or margin for payback?

Use margin. Revenue overstates the return, because food, drink and variable labour come out of every added cover. In the example, payback on revenue alone would read 5.2 months ($35,000 ÷ ($81,000 ÷ 12)); on margin before energy, 17.3 months ($35,000 ÷ ($24,300 ÷ 12)); on margin after energy, 18.3.

### What should installed cost include?

Heaters, Affinity controls, wiring or gas supply, mounting hardware, permit fees and installation labour – plus storage for propane if you use it.
