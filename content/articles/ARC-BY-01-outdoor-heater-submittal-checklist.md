A complete outdoor heater submittal brings together the product data and installation manual, a coverage layout, the electrical load for each heater with its circuit and breaker schedule, recess or mounting details with framing dimensions, clearance data, a controls schematic showing who wires what, and the certification marks. When you review it, three questions carry most of the weight: whether each heater has a sized, dedicated circuit, whether the structure has its opening and blocking drawn, and whether every control has a wire, a location and an owner.

## Coordinating Heater Circuits

Each fixed electric heater is best treated as a continuous load. The NEC's fixed electric space-heating rules size branch-circuit conductors at not less than 125% of the heater load[^s3]. Ask the electrical engineer to confirm which article governs each unit (Article 424 for fixed space heating, or Article 422 for appliances) under the edition the jurisdiction has adopted, and to show it on the panel schedule[^s3][^s4].

A few points tend to generate RFIs when they are left open:

- **Supply voltage.** Bromic lists 208V, 240V and 277V supply options for commercial sites[^s1]. Confirm the building service and the model voltage together, since a 240V heater on a 208V service delivers less output. [[VERIFY: output and coverage derating at 208V per model — Product team]]
- **Dedicated breakers.** Show one circuit per heater, or a grouping the manual permits, with the breaker size on the schedule.
- **Disconnecting means.** Locate the disconnect where the code and the manual require it, and make it visible on the drawings. [[VERIFY: NEC disconnect requirements for fixed space heaters in the adopted edition — electrical engineer]]
- **Conduit and boxes.** Route conduit into the soffit or beam before the ceiling closes, using wiring methods and boxes rated for damp or wet locations as the exposure requires.
- **Ground-fault protection.** Confirm any GFCI or ground-fault requirement under the adopted NEC edition. [[VERIFY: ground-fault requirements for hardwired outdoor heaters by NEC edition — electrical engineer]]

## Framing For A Seamless Recessed Heater

Recess kits flush-mount Platinum and Tungsten Electric heaters into roof or ceiling cavities[^s1], and the result is only as clean as the framing behind it. The opening, its trimmers and the cavity depth belong on the framing drawings, so the installer finds them waiting rather than working them out on site. Where ceilings are high, suspension poles bring heaters down to the people below; detail the structural attachment above[^s1].

At submittal, check that the cut-out dimensions match the kit, that cavity depth and any required ventilation or clearance inside the recess match the manual, that the ceiling finish returns cleanly to the kit's trim, and that the structure carries the heater weight. The Tungsten Select and Coastal collections (coming soon) are designed for low-clearance recessing, so confirm their dimensions before detailing[^s2].

## Clearances That Keep Every Installation Right

Bromic's guidance sets minimum mounting heights of 72 in above floor for wall mounts and 96 in for ceiling mounts, varying with output[^s1]. Clearances to combustible materials, to walls and to the ceiling come from each model's installation manual, and should always be taken from that manual rather than from a spec template or another product. Mark the clearance envelope on the RCP and on sections through pergolas, awnings, umbrellas and planting.

## Coordinating The Controls

Every Bromic heater is paired with Affinity Smart-Heat® Controls, and the controls belong in the submittal as part of the heater itself. Each heater on the drawings needs:

- An Affinity Smart-Heat® controller (On/Off, Dimmer, or the Eclipse controller for Eclipse heaters).
- A manual wall switch.
- The Bromic Smart-Heat app for zones, schedules and timers. Wi-Fi and app features require compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately[^s2].

For Control4, Crestron or Lutron, compatibility varies by system and may need additional integration hardware or configuration[^s2]. Name who supplies the interface, who writes the programming and who provides the network drop at the controller. Show the controller location on the electrical plan, sheltered as the manual requires, along with the circuit-to-zone mapping on the schedule.

## FAQ

### What should be in a submittal package for outdoor heaters?
Product data, the installation manual, a coverage layout, the electrical schedule, mounting or recess details, a controls schematic, certification and warranty.

### Do outdoor electric heaters need a dedicated circuit?
It is wise to plan for one. Fixed electric space-heating loads are treated as continuous and sized at 125%[^s3], and the manual and the electrical engineer set the final circuiting.

### Which NEC article applies to outdoor infrared heaters?
Usually Article 424 for fixed electric space heating[^s3]. The electrical engineer confirms this against the adopted edition and the unit's listing.

### Who wires the controls?
The electrician wires the line-voltage connections and controllers, and an integrator handles Control4, Crestron or Lutron programming. Assign both on the drawings so nothing is left to chance.
