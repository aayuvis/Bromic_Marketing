Restaurants keep outdoor dining open in winter by warming the guest directly. Radiant (infrared) heaters land their warmth on people and tables, so they keep working in a light breeze; add a partial cover and some wind screening, and a patio that once closed in October can welcome diners on most cool evenings.[^s3] The decision is a revenue one, because every heated seat is a table you can fill on a night it would otherwise sit empty.

## Why Patio Tables Fall Quiet For Half The Year

Guests feel the chill long before the kitchen closes. For many restaurants the patio is a large share of capacity and of income. In a 2021 National Restaurant Association survey, 65% of operators offered outdoor dining, and 35% of those said it made up more than 40% of average daily sales – 41% among full-service restaurants.[^s1] Demand has held, too: the Association's 2023 industry report found 69% of adults like the option to eat outside.[^s2] [[VERIFY: confirm the 69% figure in the NRA 2023 State of the Restaurant Industry report itself, or cut it — Marketing]]

As the evening cools, those seats stop earning, and the loss is a quiet one. Nobody complains; the host simply steers guests indoors, or the patio closes for the season.

## What Keeps A Guest Warm Under The Open Sky

Outdoors, warm air rises and drifts away on the breeze. Forced-air heating cannot hold a temperature in an open space, and a thermostat has nothing steady to read. Radiant heat behaves much like sunshine, settling on people and surfaces.[^s3]

Radiant heat comes in different wavelengths, and the one you choose shapes the evening your guests remember: warmth that reaches the far end of the table, soft light over the plates, no glare. During service, warm-up time matters. A heater that is warm the moment a table is seated can be switched on zone by zone through the evening, instead of running from open to close.

## Shaping The Space For Warmth

Most patios can be heated beautifully when the design is right. Bromic treats a space as outdoor when at least 30% of its perimeter is permanently open. A partial cover – an awning, pergola or soffit – holds a "heat cloud" that adds a convective boost on top of the radiant warmth.[^s3]

- **Covered terraces and awnings:** mount electric heaters on the wall or soffit, clear of the path your servers take.
- **Open courtyards and sidewalks:** freestanding heaters move with the layout; allow one for every four seats.
- **Rooftops:** the wind has the most say up high, so screen the windward side and choose heat that carries to the table, such as medium wave [[VERIFY: medium wave test data for distance heat travels — Product team]].

## Keeping Running Costs In Step With Your Covers

The most effective way to manage running cost is to warm only the seats in use. Outdoor heat cannot be thermostatically controlled, so your controls become the lever that matters:[^s3]

1. Divide the patio into zones that follow your sections and servers.
2. Schedule each zone to the reservation book rather than the clock.
3. Use output steps – Eclipse runs at 33, 66 or 100% – to suit the night.[^s4]
4. Switch a zone off once its last table leaves.

Fuel choice shapes the cost as well. Propane is inexpensive to start with and costly to run and handle, while natural gas and electric balance differently. [The five-year cost comparison](item:COM-CO-01) sets them side by side.

## Knowing It Will Pay

Start by putting a figure on the empty nights. The method is straightforward: heated seats × turns × average check × extra nights × the share of those seats you actually fill. Set that against the installed cost and the running cost, and you have a payback in months. [The ROI article](item:COM-IN-01) walks through it with every input labelled.

## FAQ

### Can a restaurant patio stay open all winter?

On many nights, yes, with radiant heat, a partial cover and wind screening. In very cold or windy climates, expect a shorter extension and plan more heaters per square foot. Bromic's design service can model your site for you.

### Do patio heaters work in wind?

Radiant heaters do, within limits, because they warm people directly. Long-wave heat is the most affected by wind.[^s3] Screening the windward side helps any heater.

### Electric or gas for a restaurant patio?

Electric suits covered, fixed installations and avoids fuel handling, while gas suits large open areas with a gas supply. Propane brings tank storage and exchange labour. Compare the five-year cost alongside the purchase price.

### How many heaters does a patio need?

For overhead heaters, divide the seating area (about 20 ft² a seat) by each heater's coverage adjusted for cover and climate, and round up: 40 seats, partly covered on cool evenings, is 800 ÷ (143 × 0.85) = 6.6, so seven Platinum 4500W heaters with Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off or Dimmer controls and a wall switch. Freestanding heaters serve no more than four seats each, so the same 40 seats need ten. See [how many heaters for a 40-seat patio](item:COM-IN-KB1).

### Do I need a permit for patio heaters?

Often. Hard-wired electric heaters usually need an electrical permit, gas lines a plumbing or fuel-gas permit, and portable gas heaters must meet the fire code. Confirm with your local building and fire authority.
