# Growing Your Outdoor Heating Work

Outdoor rooms have become part of the home, and the people who live in them want to stay out long after dark. The contractor who designs the circuit, connects the controls and hands over a system that works beautifully the first time is the one every builder, designer and homeowner calls again.

![A black Tungsten heater on suspension poles above a restaurant dining room that opens to the outdoors](img:d-tungsten-suspended-restaurant)

## The Work Is Already On The Plan

60.5% of single-family homes started in 2025 came with a patio, up from 59.6% in 2019,[^s1] and 86% of buyers rate a patio essential or desirable.[^s2] Far fewer homes come with the warmth to enjoy it once the sun goes down, because only 23% of homeowners prioritise outdoor heating when they plan the space.[^s3] Raise heat at the first site walk and price the pre-wire with the patio, and the job is yours before anyone else is asked.

You design the electrical system that powers the space, install it safely and keep it running efficiently for years. We make that easier, with the right heater for the space, the controls to run it and people who answer the phone when you have a question on site.

![Two installers fitting a recessed heater into a timber-lined ceiling](img:d-installers-recessing)

## 1. Getting The Code Right From The Start

A job that passes inspection the first time is the job that earns the next referral. Bromic electric heaters are installed to ANSI/NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code, as your state and city adopt it.[^s4][^s11] A job that misses it costs you twice, first in red tags, stop-work orders and repeat visits, and then in the referral that never comes.

**Getting It Right The First Time**

These are the details we most often see trip up an outdoor job:

- Heaters fed from a shared circuit instead of their own, so the breaker trips on the first cold night.
- Cable runs left unprotected where a patio will see heavy use.
- Heaters mounted below the minimum height. Bromic's guidance is at least 72 in above the floor for a wall mount and at least 96 in for a ceiling mount; it varies with output, so take the figure from the installation manual for the model.[^s4]
- Supply voltage left unchecked against the heater. A 240 V heater on a 208 V supply gives less heat than the client was promised.
- Controls left until the end, with no box or cable run planned for them.

**How We Help You Get It Right**

- **A free heating design plan** for every project, with heater selection and positions on the client's plan, coverage, and a parts and controls list you can price from.[^s7][^s10]
- **Installation manuals and CAD and Revit/BIM files** for every heater, from the technical resources page.[^s10]
- **Technical support** from people who know the products, on 1-800-301-1293, Monday to Friday, 9 am to 4 pm Pacific.[^s7]
- **The pre-wire guide** ([asset:A16](asset:A16)), covering circuits, boxes, blocking, recess openings and control runs for every heater type, with a hand-off sheet.

Codes vary by location. Confirm every installation with the local building authority and the authority having jurisdiction; electrical work must be carried out by a licensed electrician. The primary source is NFPA 70 as adopted locally.[^s11]

## 2. Connecting To The Systems Your Clients Already Have

On most outdoor jobs, the real craft lies in fitting new equipment into the electrical system and the smart home the client already has. Bromic heaters and Affinity Smart-Heat Controls are engineered to make that part straightforward.

**Engineered For The Supply You Have**

Tungsten Smart-Heat® Electric runs on 220–240 V and 208 V, and the 4000 W and 6000 W models also run on 277 V, the commercial supply many restaurants and hotels have.[^s5] Commercial projects can choose 208 V, 240 V or 277 V supply.[^s7] Match the heater to the supply at the design stage, well before the day of install.

**Recommended Controls & Accessories**

Every Bromic heater is paired with Affinity Smart-Heat® Controls, so the warmth is always exactly where, and when, your client wants it. Every recommendation in this guide includes them.

- **Affinity Smart-Heat On/Off controller.** Switches heaters on and off by zone, by app or wall switch. Plan for 6 kW per channel at 208–277 V; IP54 enclosure.[^s6]
- **Affinity Smart-Heat Dimmer controller.** Sets heat output by zone, so owners turn it down on mild nights. Plan for 6 kW per channel at 208–277 V.[^s6]
- **Affinity Eclipse controller.** Runs Eclipse heat and rim lighting. Plan for one Eclipse heater per channel.[^s6]
- **Manual wall switch.** Local on and off, single or dual. Plan for 20 A, 120–277 V; indoor or outdoor kit.[^s6]
- **Bromic Smart-Heat app.** Zones, schedules and timers from a phone. Plan for Wi-Fi coverage at the controller.[^s7]
- **Affinity expansion board.** Ethernet and 0–10 V for a building management or home automation system. Built into the 5-channel controller; BMS mode turns off the app.[^s6]

Wi-Fi and app features need compatible Affinity Smart-Heat Controls, sold separately. The controls connect to Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant, and to Control4, Crestron and Lutron systems; compatibility varies by system and may need extra integration hardware or configuration.[^s7] The controllers are certified to UL 60730-1 and CAN/CSA E60730-1.[^s6]

The 4000 W and 6000 W Tungsten heaters have two elements that can be controlled separately, so one heater can run at part output on a dimmer channel without a second circuit.[^s5]

**Your Checklist Before Rough-In**

- [ ] Supply voltage confirmed for every heater (208, 240 or 277 V).
- [ ] One dedicated circuit per heater or per controller channel, sized from the installation manual and the code.
- [ ] Controller location chosen: dry, reachable, inside Wi-Fi range if the app is used.
- [ ] Box and cable run to every wall switch.
- [ ] Recess framing or suspension pole fixing agreed with the builder.[^s4]
- [ ] Integration with the home or building system confirmed with the integrator.

## 3. Built For The Weather, Season After Season

Outdoor equipment lives with rain, salt air, wind and the long swing from summer to winter. A heater that fails in its second season costs you a callback and a client, so we build ours to last.

- **Tungsten Electric** is rated IPX4 against splashing water.[^s5]
- **Platinum Smart-Heat Electric Marine** is built in AISI 316 marine-grade stainless steel, rated IPX5, and carries Lloyd's Register approval for marine use, which makes it the heater to specify within reach of the coast.[^s8]
- **Tungsten Coastal** and **Tungsten Select** are available now, launched in October 2026. Ask your Bromic contact for their ratings before you specify them.
- **The Affinity On/Off controller** has an IP54 enclosure.[^s6]

## 4. Making Your Quote The One They Remember

Clients compare quotes, and most look alike, with a heater, a price and a date. With a little more care, yours stays in their mind.

- **Send a heating plan with the quote.** A Bromic design plan shows where each heater goes and the space it warms, turning a line item into a picture of the evening ahead.[^s10]
- **Quote the complete system.** Heaters, controllers, wall switch and app set-up belong in one price, because a heater is a finished job once its controls are in, and the client feels the difference on the first cold night.
- **Offer the pre-wire on every patio.** Rough-in at framing costs far less than opening a finished soffit later, and it lets the owner add heat after they move in. [[VERIFY: typical rough-in cost range from partner electricians — US field sales]]
- **Talk about safety plainly.** Dedicated circuits, the right mounting height and outdoor-rated controls build confidence, and clients trust the contractor who explains why.
- **Show it working.** A heated patio at dusk sells the next one. Ask to photograph the finished job; as a Bromic partner you can have projects featured (below).
